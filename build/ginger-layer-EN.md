## THE ONE THING

Ginger is a food-dose gut agent with a modest, timing-dependent record against nausea and a small analgesic effect, and no demonstrated systemic mechanism: free gingerols are undetectable in blood after even 2 g of extract, 100- to 1,000-fold below the concentrations the "natural ibuprofen" story needs, so the marketed anti-inflammatory, hormonal, metabolic and blood-thinning claims rest on test tubes, rodents and uncontrolled studies rather than on people.

## MYTHS VS REALITY

| The claim you've heard | What the evidence actually shows |
|---|---|
| "Ginger is a natural ibuprofen that blocks COX in your blood" | After 2 g of a 5 % extract, free 6-gingerol is undetectable and free 10-gingerol and 6-shogaol peak near 10 ng/mL (about 0.05 µM); COX-2 IC50s are 7.5 to 32 µM. In patients, gastric mucosal PGE2 rose on ginger extract where it fell on diclofenac. The mechanism has not been shown in a person. |
| "Ginger is as effective as sumatriptan for migraine" | One 100-patient trial with no placebo arm, no non-inferiority margin and no effect sizes in its abstract; the 2026 GRADE synthesis rates it "very uncertain". The only placebo-controlled prophylaxis RCT (600 mg/day, 3 months) was negative. |
| "Ginger raises testosterone by 17 %" | No randomized placebo-controlled trial of ginger on testosterone exists in men. The 17.7 % figure is an open-label pre/post change in 75 men with upper-normal baseline testosterone, no placebo and no stated dose, in a journal PubMed does not index. |
| "Ginger thins the blood like aspirin" | At 1 to 4 g/day of dried powder, no controlled study changed platelet aggregation, bleeding time or INR, including a warfarin pharmacokinetic trial. The signal comes from single 10 g doses, litres of tea, self-reported bleeding and case reports without rechallenge. |
| "Ginger stops vomiting in pregnancy" | About 1 g/day lowers nausea intensity (MD 1.20 VAS points, I² = 0 %) but has no reliable effect on vomiting episodes, and it is equivalent to vitamin B6. A 68,522-woman cohort shows no malformation or perinatal signal. |
| "The big chemo trials proved ginger does nothing" | The largest trial (576 analysed) was positive for acute nausea when 0.5 to 1 g/day was started three days before chemotherapy; two well-run trials starting 1 to 2 g on the chemo day were null. Pooled, the reproducible effect is less acute vomiting at ≤1 g/day for more than 3 days. |
| "Take ginger before your workout and it will hurt less" | Every single-dose trial is null for pain, effort, VO2 and endurance. The positive data require 2 g/day for 5 to 11 days before the damaging session, cut next-day soreness by about a quarter in one laboratory's trials, and never lowered muscle-damage enzymes. |
| "Ginger lowers blood sugar like a drug and melts fat" | In type 2 diabetes the pooled Iranian trials give HbA1c −0.4 to −1.0 points; the one risk-of-bias-filtered pool and the largest single trial found nothing. Weight falls 1 to 1.5 kg in one pool and not at all in the larger one; "thermogenesis" is 43 kcal/day in a ten-man meal test. |
| "Fresh ginger is stronger than dried, and heating makes it stronger" | Dried powder holds 4.4 to 11.3 mg/g of actives against 1.1 to 1.5 mg/g in fresh rhizome. In the only human comparison, heated ginger gave 23 % and raw 25 % soreness reduction. |
| "4 g/day is the safe upper limit set by the WHO" | The figure is the top of the German Commission E's 1990 therapeutic range, not a toxicological limit. The 2025 EU monograph sets 1 to 2 g before travel and a maximum of 2.5 g/day; no human NOAEL exists. |

## WHAT YOU ACTUALLY LEARN

- **Pharmacokinetics settle most of the argument:** gingerols and shogaols are glucuronidated on first pass, circulate as conjugates with 1 to 3 h half-lives, never accumulate, and reach free concentrations 100- to 1,000-fold below every enzyme IC50 in the mechanism story.
- **Nausea is the one bounded, reproducible use:** about 1 g/day for pregnancy nausea (not vomiting), 0.5 to 1 g/day pre-loaded before chemotherapy for acute vomiting, 1 g or more before surgery for nausea scores; more than 1 g did less, and timing mattered more than dose.
- **The gut mechanism is real and small:** 1.2 g halves liquid gastric half-emptying on ultrasound without relieving dyspepsia, the constituents inhibit the 5-HT3 receptor non-competitively at a site distinct from ondansetron's, and the one human intermediate tied to nausea is blunted gastric tachygastria and vasopressin release.
- **Pain effects are small and the comparators are weak:** dysmenorrhea improves about 1.5 cm on a 10 cm scale against placebo in small Iranian trials; osteoarthritis pain by SMD −0.30 with twice the GI withdrawals; "equal to NSAIDs" and "equal to sumatriptan" come from trials with no placebo arm and no non-inferiority design.
- **Exercise soreness is analgesia, not less damage:** eleven days of 2 g/day cut 24 h soreness by 23 to 25 % in one laboratory with borderline p-values; creatine kinase never fell and once rose longer with ginger; acute doses, heating and endurance performance are null.
- **Cardiometabolic effects are real in direction and weak in certainty:** CRP −0.4 to −1.0 mg/L in sick cohorts with no healthy-adult trial, triglycerides −9 to −25 mg/dL, blood pressure −5/−2 mmHg only where it was elevated, HbA1c positive in Iranian pools and null in the risk-of-bias-filtered pool.
- **Hormones have no controlled human evidence:** the rodent testosterone literature is toxicant rescue at human-equivalent doses of 0.5 to 11 g/day of extract; the one RCT in men found lower sperm DNA fragmentation, no change in count, and motility trending worse.
- **The one consistent harm is heartburn:** GI adverse effects are common (1 to 10 %), dose-related, grade 1 at 2 g, and doubled withdrawals in osteoarthritis trials; a daily shot holds at most 35 to 90 mg of actives, a food-range dose with no measurable bleeding, metabolic or hormonal effect.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU

- **Use ginger for what it does:** nausea, sluggish gastric emptying, period pain and post-exercise soreness, at 0.5 to 1 g/day of dried powder for nausea and 1.5 to 2 g/day for the pain and soreness protocols; expect small effects.
- **Time it, don't dose it:** take it one hour before travel, three days before chemotherapy, and for 5 to 11 days before a hard training block; a single dose on the day does nothing measurable.
- **Do not expect anti-inflammatory, hormonal or metabolic effects:** no controlled trial supports testosterone, fat-loss or drug-level blood-sugar claims, and the biomarker effects seen in sick cohorts have no healthy-adult trial behind them.
- **Stop worrying about bleeding at normal doses:** 1 to 4 g/day of powder had no platelet or INR effect in any controlled study; pause supplements before surgery as a cost-free precaution, and avoid litres of ginger tea if you take a direct oral anticoagulant.
- **Buy dried powder in capsules, not promises:** capsule powder carried every positive trial, ginger in tea did not change glycemia, supplements vary 10-fold between brands, and no trial exists for ginger ale, candied ginger or lozenges.
- **Treat a morning shot as a food:** it holds at most tens of milligrams of actives, comparable to 1 to 2 g of powder; keep it if you like it and drop it if it gives you heartburn, the one cost the trials consistently record.
- **Pregnancy and drugs:** about 1 g/day is reassuring for malformations in 68,522 women while early-loss precision is poor and the EU monograph still says "preferable to avoid"; expect additive effects with antidiabetic and antihypertensive drugs at 2 g/day, and do not combine with aprepitant without noting the one trial where nausea worsened.
