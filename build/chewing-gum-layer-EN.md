## THE ONE THING

Chewing gum is a biphasic physiological stimulant whose real, settled medical wins are mechanical and visceral — not systemic: surging saliva buffers plaque acids within 3 minutes and postoperative sham feeding accelerates bowel recovery by 10 to 13 hours, while claims of burning fat, chiseling jawlines, or boosting memory are completely refuted.

## MYTHS VS REALITY

| The claim you've heard | What the evidence actually shows |
|---|---|
| "Chewing gum burns 11 kcal/hour and melts 5 kg fat per year" | Refuted. The 11 kcal/hr claim was an invalid mathematical extrapolation from 12 minutes in an airtight hood; real net chewing cost is only 2.5–5 kcal/15 min, and an 8-week RCT (N=201) found zero weight loss (p=0.60) due to subsequent dietary compensation. |
| "Chewing hard gum or jaw exercisers remodels the jawline ('mewing')" | Biologically impossible. Adult craniofacial sutures are fully fused; a 6-month trial proved chewing training increased bite force via neural motor recruitment with 0% change in masseter thickness or bone shape. Exercisers risk severe TMJ disc displacement. |
| "Chewing gum boosts memory recall" | Refuted. Initial 2002 claims failed replication across multiple independent laboratories; chewing during sequence tasks causes motor-cognitive dual-task interference and actively impairs short-term recall. |
| "Any sugar-free gum with xylitol prevents cavities" | Dose-gated. Clinical anti-caries efficacy requires a strict threshold of 5 to 6 grams of xylitol per day split across 3+ chewing sessions. Most commercial gums contain trace amounts (<0.5 g/stick) far below this threshold. |
| "Swallowed gum stays in your stomach for seven years" | False. While synthetic gum base is indigestible, gastric Phase III migrating motor complexes propel the gum bolus through the pylorus, excreting it intact in stool within 24 to 72 hours. |
| "Chewing gum is completely harmless to chew all day" | Heavy habitual chewing (>1–2 hours/day) causes TMJ clicking, masticatory muscle fatigue, and chronic tension headaches in adolescents (86.7% resolve upon cessation; 100% relapse on rechallenge). |
| "Aspartame in gum is a major cancer hazard" | Reaching the established Acceptable Daily Intake (40 mg/kg/day) requires chewing 300 to 600 sticks of gum in a single day. A typical 5–10 stick daily habit represents only 1.7% to 3.3% of the conservative safety limit. |
| "Chewing gum cures bad breath and acid reflux" | Non-mint gum halves esophageal acid contact time in GERD; however, peppermint and spearmint relax the lower esophageal sphincter, and gum exacerbates aerophagia, increasing bloating in functional bowel disorders. |

## WHAT YOU ACTUALLY LEARN

- **The best dental evidence is mechanical, not chemical:** Salivary flow surges 3- to 10-fold and bicarbonate rises from 5 mM to 30–60 mM, accelerating plaque acid neutralization from >30 minutes to 1–3 minutes.
- **Postoperative sham feeding is a settled surgical therapy:** A Cochrane review of 81 RCTs (N=9,072) confirms gum chewing shortens time to first flatus by 10.4 hours and first bowel movement by 12.7 hours after abdominal surgery.
- **Cognitive arousal is real but strictly time-delimited:** Chewing attenuates the vigilance decrement and speeds reaction times during monotonous tasks, but pre-task arousal fades within 15–20 minutes and concurrent chewing impairs complex sequencing.
- **Weight loss from gum is an empirical failure:** Controlled feeding trials show acute snack blunting is neutralized by subconscious dietary compensation at subsequent meals, producing zero 24-hour caloric deficit.
- **Biomechanics and jaw health have hard limits:** Mastication strengthens bite force through neural adaptation without muscle hypertrophy, while excessive chewing causes TMJ derangement and chronic tension headaches.
- **Ingredient toxicology is nuanced:** Aspartame exposure is trivial, but sorbitol >10–20 g/day induces severe osmotic diarrhea, and elastomer bases shed hundreds of microplastic particles per gram into saliva within 8 minutes.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU

- **Chew sugar-free gum for 15–20 minutes immediately after meals** to neutralize plaque acids and remineralize enamel, but do not chew continuously all day.
- **Check the xylitol dose:** If using gum for dental protection, ensure you reach 5 to 6 grams of xylitol daily across 3 to 5 chewing bouts, or rely primarily on mechanical salivary stimulation.
- **Chew for 5 minutes right before an exam or focus block**, not during it, to capture the 15-minute arousal peak without suffering motor-cognitive interference during sequence-heavy tasks.
- **Never use commercial front-teeth jaw exercisers or hard resins for facial aesthetics:** They cannot change fused adult bone and generate dangerous Class III lever forces that destroy the TMJ disc.
- **Use non-mint gum for acid reflux**, and avoid chewing gum if you suffer from irritable bowel syndrome, aerophagia, or unexplained abdominal bloating.
- **Stop chewing immediately if you experience jaw clicking, temple tenderness, or recurrent headaches**, and keep all xylitol products strictly out of reach of dogs, where even small doses cause fatal hypoglycemia.
