## THE ONE THING

Extra virgin olive oil is a good default fat with a real but small phenolic bonus: a tablespoon holds 0.3 to 3.4 % of an ibuprofen tablet's worth of oleocanthal and the parent molecule has never been measured in human blood, the hard-outcome trials tested a whole Mediterranean diet rather than the oil, and the oil's own measurable effects are a lower oxidised LDL and about 3 mmHg off systolic pressure against refined olive oil.

## MYTHS VS REALITY

| The claim you've heard | What the evidence actually shows |
|---|---|
| "Oleocanthal is natural ibuprofen" | It inhibits COX-1 and COX-2 in a purified enzyme assay and stings the throat through the same TRPA1 channel, but a 13.5 g tablespoon of a high-phenolic oil holds 1.35 to 6.8 mg of it, 0.3 to 3.4 % of a 200 to 400 mg tablet. Parent oleocanthal has never been measured in human plasma; in animals it disappears because its aldehydes react with glycine. |
| "A daily shot of EVOO is anti-inflammatory" | The EVOO-only meta-analysis of 33 trials found no effect on CRP, IL-6, IL-10 or TNF-α. Reductions appear when olive oil comes inside a Mediterranean diet, and the largest oleocanthal-rich trial differed from common olive oil only on interferon-γ. |
| "EVOO works as a painkiller" | No randomised trial of EVOO or oleocanthal-rich oil on pain exists. Olive extracts in osteoarthritis pool to a modest, uncertain effect from three trials, and a six-month olive-leaf trial was null. |
| "The EU has approved olive oil polyphenols as healthy" | One claim is authorised: "olive oil polyphenols contribute to the protection of blood lipids from oxidative stress", at 5 mg of hydroxytyrosol and derivatives per 20 g of oil (250 mg/kg). It is a biomarker claim, not a heart, pain or brain claim, and the regulation names no measurement method. |
| "The polyphenol number on the label tells you your dose" | The same 30 oils qualified for the claim 1 to 21 times out of 30 depending on the laboratory protocol; Folin and LC-MS/MS results differ two- to threefold. Phenolics fall 40 to 46 % in a year at room temperature, so the label describes the oil at testing. |
| "PREDIMED proved olive oil prevents heart attacks" | PREDIMED tested a Mediterranean diet with free EVOO or free nuts against low-fat advice. After its 2018 republication the EVOO arm gave HR 0.69 and the nut arm 0.72; the benefit was mainly fewer strokes, and cardiovascular and total mortality were not clearly reduced. |
| "PREDIMED was retracted, so the evidence collapsed" | The 2013 report was retracted for randomisation failures at two of eleven sites and republished with almost unchanged estimates (0.70 became 0.69). It is now partly observational, and it still cannot separate the oil from the diet. |
| "Olive oil lowers cholesterol" | Only when it replaces saturated fat such as butter, lard or ghee. Added on top of a diet it barely moves LDL (+0.04 mg/dL per 10 g/day), and corn, safflower and rapeseed oils lower LDL and apoB more than olive oil. |
| "Oleocanthal clears amyloid from the brain" | The P-glycoprotein and LRP1 clearance mechanism comes from mouse studies by one laboratory network with commercial ties, mostly with injected pure compound. The MIND trial supplied about two tablespoons of EVOO a day for three years and found no cognitive or MRI benefit. |
| "Never cook with EVOO; its smoke point is too low" | Smoke point mainly tracks free acidity. In frying tests EVOO lasted longer than seed oils before reaching the polar-compound discard limit and formed fewer aldehydes. Heat does remove 40 to 75 % of its phenolics in a sauté. |

## WHAT YOU ACTUALLY LEARN

- **The pharmacokinetics decide the ibuprofen story:** hydroxytyrosol and tyrosol are absorbed but circulate about 98 % conjugated, free hydroxytyrosol peaks near 0.03 µM after 25 mL, and the cell studies used 10 to 100 µM of a parent compound that has not been found in human plasma.
- **The one reproducible acute effect is on platelets:** 40 mL of oleocanthal-rich oil dampened ex vivo platelet aggregation for a few hours in trials of 9 and 10 people, and the authors of the first trial concluded it did not run through COX.
- **The phenolic-specific effects are small and real:** against refined olive oil, oxidised LDL falls (SMD −0.25 to −0.68), systolic pressure falls about 3 mmHg, HDL rises 1 to 2 mg/dL and LDL falls 4 to 5 mg/dL only at very high phenolic doses.
- **The hard outcomes belong to the diet:** PREDIMED and CORDIOPREV tested whole Mediterranean patterns built on EVOO; CORDIOPREV gave 9.6 fewer events per 1,000 person-years in coronary men against a real low-fat diet, with olive-oil-foundation co-funding.
- **Cohorts show a curve that flattens early:** olive oil intake tracks 15 to 20 % lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, with most of it reached by about 18 to 22 g/day; in the US cohorts the advantage is over butter, margarine and mayonnaise, not over other vegetable oils.
- **The label is a moving target:** phenolic content runs from near zero in refined oil to several thousand mg/kg in rare Greek oils, premium oils average 343 to 421 mg/kg, and storage, light, oxygen and heat all erode it.
- **Brain claims are mouse claims:** at least three laboratories report less amyloid and tau in transgenic mice, but the human trials are small pilots and the largest trial that supplied daily EVOO was null.
- **Safety at food doses is good:** no documented allergy to the oil itself, no warfarin interaction study, and contaminants concentrated in pomace oil; retail grade fraud mainly costs the phenolic dose.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU

- **Use EVOO as your default added fat:** the lipid and cohort benefits are largest when it replaces butter, margarine, mayonnaise or other saturated and processed fats, not when it is added on top.
- **Take the phenolic dose raw:** a hot sauté removes about three-quarters of the phenolics, so dressings, finishing and an uncooked spoon keep the most; cooking with it is still safe.
- **Buy for freshness and a stated method:** choose the latest harvest date, a phenolic value that names its method and test date, and tin, bag-in-box or other opaque packaging; finish an opened bottle within weeks.
- **Raise the dose with a better oil, not a bigger pour:** a tablespoon of a typical good EVOO carries about 3 to 7 mg of phenolics and 120 kcal; a higher-phenolic oil gives more phenolics for the same calories.
- **Do not expect a painkiller, an anti-inflammatory or a brain treatment:** no human trial at a tablespoon dose supports those uses, and pure oleocanthal supplements have no human safety or efficacy data.
- **There is no evidence for a fasted shot:** no trial compared an empty-stomach dose with oil taken with food, and a fasted bolus contracts the gallbladder; if you have gallstones, take it with a meal.
- **Mention a switch to very high-oleocanthal oil if you take antiplatelet drugs or anticoagulants:** the platelet effect is measurable at 40 mL, and no study has tested it on top of those drugs.
