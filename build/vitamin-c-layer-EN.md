## THE ONE THING

Vitamin C is a 200 mg nutrient sold as a gram-dose medicine: plasma saturates at food doses (68 µmol/L on 200 mg a day, 85 on 2,500), white cells are full at 100 to 400 mg, anything above a 500 mg dose is increasingly excreted, and the gram-dose trials are null for colds, hearts, cancer and training while raising men's kidney-stone risk and, by vein, harming septic and COVID-19 patients.

## MYTHS VS REALITY

| The claim you've heard | What the evidence actually shows |
|---|---|
| "More is better; 1,000 mg keeps my levels high" | In the NIH depletion-repletion study, 200 mg a day was the first dose past the steep part of the curve and complete plasma saturation came at 1,000 mg; raising intake from 200 to 2,500 mg lifted fasting plasma only from 68 to 85 µmol/L. Above a 500 mg dose absorption falls and the surplus leaves in urine, and oxalate and urate excretion are already higher at 1,000 mg. |
| "Vitamin C arms your immune cells" | Neutrophils, monocytes and lymphocytes were saturated at 100 mg a day in men and 400 in women, at 14 times the plasma concentration. Two gold kiwifruit a day took young men above 70 µmol/L in a week. The cells are full at food intake. |
| "Pauling proved megadoses prevent colds" | Pauling's 2.3 g optimum was an evolutionary argument, his 1974 band ran from 250 to 4,000 mg, and he ran no trial. The 2,349-person Toronto trial found no significant effect in any of eight arms and no dose-response from 0.25 to 2 g. Cochrane pools 10,708 community participants to an incidence ratio of 0.97 (0.94 to 1.00). |
| "Take vitamin C at the first sneeze" | With daily use colds are about 8 % shorter in adults and 14 % in children, roughly half a day on a week-long cold. Started at onset, seven comparisons covering 3,249 episodes show no consistent effect, and the best modern trial at 3 g was null. The 6 to 8 g argument rests on two 1970s trials their own authors read as negative, and two supporting meta-analyses have been retracted. |
| "Antioxidants help you recover" | Across 50 trials, antioxidant supplements shave 0.2 to 0.5 cm off a 10 cm soreness scale, below the 1.4 cm a person can notice. Vitamin C alone protected neither force nor range of motion in any trial, and 1 g after downhill running delayed the recovery of muscle function. |
| "Vitamin C blunts your training, so never take it" | The famous blunting trials combined vitamin C with vitamin E. Pooled across 18 trials the combination changed neither VO2max, endurance, lean mass nor strength. Marker blunting of PGC-1α, COX4 and SOD is real at 1 g of C with E, and the one outcome cost is a 1.4 % against 3.9 % lean-mass gain in 60- to 81-year-old men. The vitamin-C-only data are five supplemented men, a null trial and a 1.3 ± 1.3 % effect. |
| "Vitamin C protects the heart" | 500 mg a day for eight years in 14,641 physicians gave cardiovascular events HR 0.99; the same dose for 9.4 years in 8,171 high-risk women gave RR 1.02. A 2 g dose dilates sick arteries for a couple of hours; 1 g a day for eight weeks in smokers held plasma at 92 µmol/L and changed nothing. Blood pressure falls 4/1.5 mmHg in eight-week trials and not at all over five years. |
| "People with high vitamin C live longer, so take it" | They do, by about 20 % per 20 µmol/L of plasma ascorbate. The signal is strongest when measured in blood and weakest as intake, disappears in Mendelian randomisation, and its own authors read it as a fruit-and-vegetable marker. The pill has never reproduced it: vitamin C alone carries no mortality signal across 29 trials. |
| "It's water-soluble, so 1,000 to 2,000 mg is harmless" | A gram of ascorbate makes 6 to 13 mg of extra urinary oxalate a day; 2 g raised oxalate 20 % in healthy people and 33 % in stone formers. Men taking 1,000 mg or more had RR 1.41 for symptomatic stones in HPFS, HR 1.43 in the pooled Harvard cohorts and HR 1.92 among Swedish supplement users. Women and food-only intake showed nothing. The 2,000 mg upper level was set on diarrhoea in 2000. |
| "IV vitamin C treats cancer, so a pill must help" | Oral dosing tops out near 220 µmol/L; infusions reach 13,000 to 49,000, which is where the peroxide mechanism works. Every phase I trial found zero single-agent responses; the only randomised blinded tests of oral megadoses, 10 g a day, were null. The evidence for benefit is one open-label 34-patient trial awaiting replication. |
| "High-dose vitamin C saves septic and COVID patients" | LOVIT randomised 872 septic adults to 50 mg/kg every six hours: death or persistent organ dysfunction 44.5 % against 38.5 %, RR 1.21. The 2,590-patient COVID-19 trials were stopped for harm and futility. The HAT-protocol trials were null. |
| "Whole-food, Ester-C or liposomal vitamin C is absorbed better" | Kiwifruit-derived and synthetic vitamin C were identical in plasma, white cells and muscle at steady state; the "bioflavonoids add 35 %" claim is one eight-person single-dose study from 1988. Ester-C matches plain ascorbate in plasma and is gentler on an acid-sensitive stomach. Liposomal forms raise a single dose's exposure and no study has measured whether the extra is kept or excreted. |
| "Nobody in a rich country is deficient" | About 7 % of US adults are below 11.4 µmol/L, unchanged since 2003; a quarter of low-income British men were; hospital inpatients run 28 %. Smokers' plasma is a third lower and they need roughly double the intake, as do people over about 100 kg. |

## WHAT YOU ACTUALLY LEARN

- **The curve is the whole story:** the steep part of the dose-to-plasma curve lies between 30 and 100 mg a day, 200 mg is the first dose beyond it, and 2,500 mg buys 17 µmol/L more than 200. The renal threshold sits at 72 to 86 µmol/L, and above it clearance approaches the glomerular filtration rate.
- **Oral and intravenous are different drugs:** 1.25 g by mouth peaks at 135 µmol/L and the same dose by vein at 885; 50 g by vein reaches 13,400. Nothing a pill does happens at the concentrations the intravenous literature depends on.
- **The requirement debate is 75 to 200 mg on the same data:** leukocyte saturation gave the Institute of Medicine 75 to 90 mg, a 50 µmol/L mass balance gave the German societies 110 and 95 mg, and the plasma curve gave the NIH group 200 mg. Every camp's number is a piece of fruit.
- **Status has bands and one valid test:** deficiency below 11.4 µmol/L, marginal at or below 23, adequate at or above 50, saturating above 65; fasting plasma in a promptly stabilised EDTA tube is the measure, and baseline status and body weight shift the whole curve.
- **The one large cold effect is in people under brief heavy exertion:** RR 0.48 in 598 marathon runners, skiers and sub-arctic soldiers at 0.6 to 1 g a day, from small old trials; a 674-recruit trial at 2 g was null.
- **Low plasma ascorbate is the population a pill moves:** men in the bottom tenth gained 5.4 mL/kg/min of VO2max and 18.7 Nm of torque from 1 g for 30 days, men below 45 µmol/L had fewer colds, and low-baseline office workers lost fatigue. All of it is repletion, and 200 mg a day does it in two weeks.
- **The stone mechanism is quantified and not an assay artefact:** 1.2 to 1.8 % of a dose's millimoles become oxalate, the controlled studies used preservatives, EDTA and isotopes to exclude in-vitro conversion, and 40 % of people are high responders. On HPFS rates the men's excess is about one extra stone per 80 men a decade.
- **Oxalate nephropathy needs two of three:** intravenous grams, kidneys that cannot clear oxalate, or a gut that over-absorbs it. Oral cases carry the second factor, at doses as low as 680 mg with chronic diarrhoea.
- **Iron is a meal-level effect:** about 50 mg of vitamin C in the same meal maximises non-haem iron absorption, the whole-diet effect is small, adding it to iron tablets adds nothing, and food ascorbate equals crystals.
- **The claims that fail outright:** rebound scurvy (two subjects' urine dipped for ten days), B12 destruction (an assay artefact refuted in 1976), the warfarin interaction (one 1971 case, one 1975 negative study), prevention of cataract, macular degeneration or dementia, and collagen-building in replete adults.
- **Vitamin C alone is cleared of the antioxidant mortality signal:** across 29 low-bias trials RR 1.02 (0.98 to 1.07), while β-carotene, vitamin A and vitamin E raised mortality.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU

- **Keep vitamin C in the food column:** if you do not smoke and eat fruit or vegetables several times a day, your plasma sits at 50 to 80 µmol/L and your cells are full; a pill buys about 10 µmol/L and a few hours of higher urine.
- **Know your 200 mg:** two gold kiwifruit, or a red pepper with an orange. Boiling vegetables costs 20 to 60 % of their vitamin C; raw fruit and pepper cost nothing.
- **Bend the rule for status, not dose:** if you smoke, weigh over about 100 kg, eat little produce or test below 50 µmol/L, you are a repletion case at 100 to 200 mg a day from food or a small tablet, not a gram.
- **The one defensible pill use case:** a short course of 0.5 to 1 g a day around an ultramarathon, a ski camp or similar exertion in cold or crowded conditions.
- **If you take a pill anyway, keep it to 200 to 400 mg:** that is the range in which no renal signal appeared in any cohort band, and do not pair it with vitamin E at 1 g during a training block.
- **Men: a gram a day is a stone risk with no offsetting benefit** at that dose in a non-deficient adult.
- **Never gram doses if you have:** a prior calcium-oxalate stone, kidney disease or dialysis, gastric bypass or other malabsorption, chronic diarrhoea, G6PD deficiency (for infusions), iron overload, or chemotherapy without your oncologist's agreement.
- **Do not expect it to prevent or treat a cold:** daily use shortens a cold by about half a day; starting at onset has no consistent effect.
- **Stop supplements for a few days before a guaiac stool test:** ascorbate causes false negatives.
- **To know rather than guess:** ask for a fasting plasma ascorbate in a promptly stabilised EDTA tube; at or above 50 µmol/L is adequate by every definition in these sources, above 65 saturating.
