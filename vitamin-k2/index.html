    Vitamin K2 (MK-7): What It Actually Does vs What People Think¶

    A genuine cofactor that reliably fixes a status biomarker but has never been shown to fix a hard outcome — the clearest "the lab number moves, the disease doesn't" case in the supplement world, and weaker on outcomes than even vitamin D.

    Independent research, 30 August 2026. 85 cited sources (81 PubMed-verified — every PMID title-matched against PubMed; 1 wrong caught and corrected). Not medical advice.

    Start here¶

    Bottom line

    Vitamin K2 reliably fixes a lab marker and reliably fails to change the disease. It genuinely activates Matrix Gla Protein, the body's most potent brake on artery calcification, and a real slice of people are functionally low — so the biomarker (dp-ucMGP) drops 40-50% every time you supplement. But not one trial has shown it reverses arterial plaque, prevents a fracture, or raises testosterone, and the largest, most rigorous, independent trial was flatly null on calcification despite crushing the marker. It is safe and cheap, and pairing it with D3 is a reasonable hedge — just don't expect the marketing.

    Myths vs. reality

    The claim you've heard What the evidence actually shows

    What you actually learn

    • The biomarker moves, the disease doesn't. K2 drops dp-ucMGP 40-50% in every trial — and the calcification, fracture and event endpoints stay null. It is the purest "fixing the lab number isn't fixing you" case in the supplement aisle.
    • "Reverses plaque" is refuted. Not one trial reported regression; the independent AVADEC aortic-valve trial was null, and no heart-attack or mortality trial exists.
    • The fracture case collapsed. The eye-catching numbers came from a Japanese 45 mg drug dose, and removing the research-misconduct-tainted trials dissolved the effect; independent Western trials fix the marker and are null on bone.
    • The D3 pairing is plausible, not proven. "K2 directs the calcium" and "D3 alone calcifies your arteries" are mechanism-marketing, not human evidence.
    • Very safe, with one real exception — the warfarin interaction. And menadione (K3), the genuinely toxic form, is not the same thing as MK-4/MK-7.

    What this means for you

    • Food beats a pill for K2 — natto (fermented soy) is by far the richest source; cheese, egg yolk and liver supply less.
    • If you supplement: all-trans MK-7, a modest dose (~90-180 µg) already maxes out the biomarker, taken with a fatty meal (ideally alongside D3). Product quality matters far more than any toxicity worry — much commercial MK-7 tests below its label.
    • Pair it with D3 if you like — cheap, safe, plausible — but expect a corrected biomarker, not de-calcified arteries or stronger bones.
    • Don't take it for reversing plaque, preventing fractures, or boosting testosterone — the evidence isn't there.
    • On warfarin? Never self-start K2 — it lowers your INR and raises clot risk. Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) are unaffected.

    TL;DR

    Vitamin K2 is a real functional cofactor — it activates Matrix Gla Protein (the body's most potent inhibitor of vascular calcification) and osteocalcin in bone — and a genuine slice of people are functionally K-insufficient. K2 supplements reliably fix that biomarker: dephospho-uncarboxylated MGP falls 40-50% in every trial. But fixing the marker has not fixed the disease. "Reverses arterial plaque" is refuted — not one trial showed regression, and the largest, most rigorous, independent trial (on aortic-valve calcification) was flatly null despite crushing the biomarker; no cardiovascular event or mortality trial exists. Bone density moves modestly and inconsistently, and there is no trustworthy fracture evidence — the famous Japanese data used a 45 mg drug dose and was partly built on research-misconduct retractions. The "you MUST pair K2 with D3 or the D3 calcifies your arteries" story is mechanism-marketing; "high-dose D3 alone calcifies arteries" is unsupported in humans. "K2 boosts testosterone" is a rat study with the mechanism running backwards. It is very safe — except the warfarin interaction, which is real.

    What this review covered¶

    Vitamin K2 (menaquinone) is sold as the missing cofactor that "keeps calcium out of your arteries and puts it in your bones," a mandatory partner for vitamin D3, and a booster for bone, heart and even testosterone. A seven-cluster evidence sweep — biochemistry and forms, bone, cardiovascular, the D3+K2 pairing, kidney/diabetes/other outcomes, safety and warfarin, and the marketing myths — tested each claim against the peer-reviewed literature: the pivotal randomized trials, the meta-analyses, the large cohorts, and the regulatory positions. Every PubMed citation was title-matched against PubMed before use, and one wrong agent-supplied identifier was caught and corrected. The shape that emerges is the same family as the vitamin D, magnesium and omega-3 stories — a real biomarker that supplements move, and outcomes that don't follow — taken here to its purest extreme.

    Tier 1 — The strongest evidence¶

    The biochemistry is settled and genuinely elegant. Vitamin K is the cofactor for the enzyme that activates the "Gla-proteins" — the clotting factors (its classical, life-or-death role), osteocalcin in bone, and Matrix Gla Protein (MGP) in the vessel wall; the recycling enzyme it depends on, VKOR, is exactly what warfarin blocks1. MGP is the most potent natural inhibitor of vascular calcification known: mice engineered without it calcify all their arteries and die of aortic rupture within about two months2, and warfarin — which inactivates MGP — causes arterial calcification in rats and in humans34. This is why the hypothesis that K2 protects arteries is legitimate. The problem is everything downstream of the mechanism.

    K2 reliably fixes the vitamin-K status biomarker — that part is not in doubt. Across bone, vascular, kidney and even COVID trials, MK-7 supplementation drives the functional markers (dephospho-uncarboxylated MGP, undercarboxylated osteocalcin) down by roughly 40-50% every single time5678. Target engagement is the one thing the literature does consistently.

    But fixing the biomarker has not fixed the outcome — and that is the whole story. The pivotal, largest, independent trial — AVADEC, which gave men with aortic-valve calcification MK-7 720 µg plus vitamin D for two years — crushed the biomarker and found no difference in calcification progression (p=0.64)7. The same "marker moves, disease doesn't" split repeats in dialysis (Trevasc-HDK, iPACK-HD) and in chronic-kidney-disease stiffness (K4Kidneys)91011. This is the textbook surrogate-versus-outcome disconnect.

    MK-4 is not MK-7 — and low-dose MK-4 is a waste. At a 420 µg nutritional dose, MK-4 (half-life about one hour) is undetectable in the blood at every timepoint, while MK-7 (half-life about three days) is well absorbed and accumulates121314. MK-4's only real evidence exists at the 45 mg pharmacological drug dose — roughly 250-500 times a supplement — so a common low-dose MK-4 supplement has no pharmacokinetic rationale, and the two evidence bases must never be pooled. A further nuance: the body makes MK-4 endogenously from dietary K1 via the enzyme UBIAD11516, so K2's status as "essential and separate from K1" is softer than marketed.

    No official body sets a K2 intake, and "everyone is deficient" is a marker-based overstatement. Both EFSA (Adequate Intake 70 µg/day) and the US Institute of Medicine (120/90 µg/day) set values for phylloquinone (K1) only, explicitly calling the menaquinone evidence insufficient, and neither sets an upper limit1718. Frank, bleeding deficiency is rare; extra-hepatic under-carboxylation is common but is a surrogate, not a disease.

    Safe — with one hard exception. No Tolerable Upper Intake Level exists for vitamin K because no toxic dose has ever been found; MK-4 45 mg/day has been used for years in Japan and high-dose MK-7 trials report only mild stomach upset1719. The exception is the warfarin / vitamin-K-antagonist interaction: because these drugs work by blocking vitamin-K recycling, even a 45 µg MK-7 supplement measurably lowers INR and raises clot risk, and MK-7's long half-life makes it more disruptive than dietary K12013. A warfarin patient must never self-start K2. Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) act downstream of vitamin K and are unaffected21. And menadione (vitamin K3) — a synthetic compound that depletes glutathione and causes hemolysis, withdrawn from human use — is a different substance whose toxicity says nothing about MK-4/MK-722; the newborn "vitamin K shot" is K1, not K223.

    Tier 2 — Real but modest, surrogate-level, or population-specific¶

    "K2 reverses arterial plaque" is refuted. Not a single randomized trial has reported net regression of existing arterial or valve calcium. AVADEC was null on aortic valve7; a diabetes trial using an ¹⁸F-fluoride PET scan actually trended the wrong way24. The honest ceiling is "slower progression in some groups": the newest coronary-calcium trial (VitaK-CAC, MK-7 360 µg, 180 patients) found slower calcium progression over two years25, and a 2023 meta-analysis found a tiny pooled effect (−17 calcium units)26 — but the AVADEC coronary substudy was null on its primary endpoint27, and arterial stiffness improved in only one industry-funded trial's high-risk subgroup6 against two independent nulls1128. A systematic review concluded vitamin K does not consistently prevent calcification, stiffness or atherosclerosis29.

    No cardiovascular event or mortality trial exists. Every K2 vascular RCT is powered for an imaging or stiffness surrogate. The only event-level signal is an underpowered, exploratory composite in one substudy27 — not enough to support any efficacy claim. The observational signal that seeded the whole field — the Rotterdam Study's finding that high dietary menaquinone tracked lower coronary-disease mortality30 — is confounded (K2 comes from cheese and dairy, which track diet and lifestyle), and tellingly, in the Bruneck cohort poor K status predicted death but not heart attacks or sudden cardiac death31, the opposite of what a "keeps calcium out of your coronaries" story predicts. This is the same biomarker-predicts-death-but-the-supplement-doesn't-fix-it pattern seen with omega-3, vitamin D and magnesium.

    Bone: the density moves modestly, the fracture data collapses. Vitamin K reliably lowers undercarboxylated osteocalcin (the bone K marker) — but osteocalcin-knockout mice have denser bone, so that marker is a hypothesis, not a proven fracture pathway. At nutritional MK-7 doses, the flagship trial slowed density loss at spine and femoral neck (not total hip) over three years, with no fracture endpoint and manufacturer funding5. The famous fracture reductions come almost entirely from Japanese MK-4 45 mg drug-dose trials, most open-label32, and two things demolish them: the largest, most rigorous MK-4 fracture trial (the "OF study," 4,378 women) was NEGATIVE on its primary endpoint33, and several trials in the pooled meta-analyses came from a researcher subject to one of medicine's largest research-misconduct records — re-running the analysis without the discredited trials dissolved the anti-fracture effect3435. The most current independent synthesis rates the evidence low-quality and cannot confirm a fracture benefit36. Independent Western trials using K1 (ECKO, Booth) fix the biomarker and are null on bone density3738. No osteoporosis guideline outside Japan recommends vitamin K.

    Functional insufficiency is genuinely common — the honest kernel. A measurable slice of apparently healthy adults, and more of the elderly, carry elevated dp-ucMGP, meaning their extra-hepatic tissues are not maximally carboxylated even with normal clotting318. That is a real, MK-7-correctable state. It is just not the same as a disease that supplementing has been shown to prevent. And in kidney/dialysis patients — where the deficit and rationale are strongest — every hard calcification endpoint is still null91028.

    Tier 3 — Weak or unproven: marketing outruns the data¶

    The D3+K2 "synergy" is mechanism-marketing. The enzymology is real — vitamin D induces the Gla-proteins and K activates them — but no human trial shows that adding K2 to D3 changes any hard outcome versus D3 alone, and there is no validated dose ratio3940. The review most cited as proof is a narrative review that itself hedges. Same-meal timing is a fat-absorption nicety, not an outcome lever.

    "High-dose D3 alone calcifies your arteries" is unsupported in humans. The Women's Health Initiative coronary-calcium substudy found no difference with calcium plus D341; the scare is extrapolated from warfarin-plus-vitamin-D rat models (where the K pathway is pharmacologically blocked)42 and a Medical Hypotheses paper43. Genuine vitamin-D toxicity is a megadose problem, not a "you forgot your K2" problem.

    Diabetes, cancer, and the rest are marker associations, not supplement proof. The osteocalcin-as-insulin-hormone rationale rests on a mouse model44 that two 2020 knockout strains failed to reproduce45, and the only human K2/glucose trial was four weeks in 42 healthy young men46. In cancer, observational menaquinone associations (prostate, lung)47 and a tiny open-label liver-cancer subgroup48 were overturned by a large phase-III trial stopped for futility49. Inflammation, osteoarthritis, exercise and COVID are cross-sectional or single-study signals that null out in the actual trials505152. And while poor K status (dp-ucMGP) predicts mortality across cohorts31, that marker rises with age, illness and kidney dysfunction — heavy reverse causation — and no K2 mortality trial exists.

    Tier 4 — Myths and refuted claims¶

    "K2 boosts testosterone" is refuted, and instructively so. The claim traces to a single study in rats and testis-tumor cells at a very high dose, with no human data53. Worse, the mechanism runs backwards: the osteocalcin-to-Leydig-cell bridge it invokes needs under-carboxylated osteocalcin — the exact form K2 removes by carboxylating it. And the whole "osteocalcin is a hormone" edifice collapsed when independent labs built new knockout mice with normal testosterone, glucose and muscle5445. There is no human evidence K2 raises testosterone.

    "You must pair K2 with vitamin D" is not an established necessity — reasonable on plausibility, dishonest as a rule3941. "The advanced form is far superior" is half-marketing: MK-7 wins for nutritional absorption, but MK-4's only real evidence is the 45 mg drug dose, and the two must not be conflated1233. "K2 cures cavities / Weston-Price's Activator X" has no controlled human caries trial, and the "Activator X = K2" identification was never peer-reviewed. "EFSA-approved for your heart" is spin: EFSA authorized only the nutrient-adequacy claims that vitamin K contributes to "maintenance of normal bones" and "normal blood coagulation"55, and it rejected the vitamin-K2 "normal function of the heart and blood vessels" claim on reassessment56. Finally, the funding pattern is the tell: the recurring positive MK-7 studies are manufacturer-linked, surrogate-endpoint and sometimes from the same cohort65, while the independently funded, hard-endpoint trials skew null711.

    Bottom line¶

    Vitamin K2 is a real cofactor with a coherent mechanism and a genuinely correctable status marker, wrapped in claims its own trials don't support. It reliably fixes dephospho-uncarboxylated MGP and just as reliably fails to move the hard endpoint — no reversal of arterial calcification, no trustworthy fracture prevention, no testosterone effect, no proven diabetes, cancer or mortality benefit, and no evidence that pairing it with D3 changes any outcome. Its safety is excellent bar the warfarin interaction, its best supplement form is all-trans MK-7 (not low-dose MK-4), and its positive literature skews manufacturer-funded and surrogate-level while the independent hard-endpoint trials skew null. If you take it, take it for what it does — correct a real biomarker and add a cheap, plausible vascular hedge — not for the marketing.

    The full findings — all 35¶

    Every conclusion this review reached, grouped by how strongly the evidence backs it. Each finding is distilled from the fully-cited evidence above.

    Tier 1 — Settled: strongest, cleanest evidence (9 findings)

    1. Vitamin K2's biochemistry is settled — it activates Gla-proteins, and Matrix Gla Protein is the body's most potent calcification inhibitor.

    Vitamin K is the obligate cofactor for γ-glutamyl carboxylase, which activates the "Gla-proteins" — clotting factors, osteocalcin (bone), and Matrix Gla Protein/MGP (vessel wall); the recycling enzyme VKOR is what warfarin blocks. MGP-knockout mice calcify their arteries and die of aortic rupture within ~2 months, and warfarin (which inactivates MGP) causes arterial calcification in rats — so the mechanism by which K matters for vessels is real and strong.

    2. K2 reliably fixes the vitamin-K status biomarker in every trial — that part is not in doubt.

    Across bone, vascular, kidney and COVID trials, MK-7 supplementation drives the functional K-status markers (dp-ucMGP, undercarboxylated osteocalcin) down by ~40-50% every time. Target engagement is the one thing the K2 literature does consistently.

    3. But fixing the biomarker has NOT fixed the outcome — the defining pattern of the whole field.

    The pivotal independent aortic-valve trial (AVADEC) crushed dp-ucMGP yet showed no difference in calcification, and the same "marker moves, disease doesn't" split repeats in dialysis (Trevasc-HDK, iPACK-HD) and CKD stiffness (K4Kidneys). This is the textbook surrogate-vs-outcome disconnect — the same trap seen with omega-3-fish-oil/index, vitamin-d and magnesium.

    4. MK-4 ≠ MK-7 — low-dose MK-4 is undetectable in serum, so MK-7 is the only sensible supplement form.

    At a 420 µg nutritional dose MK-4 was undetectable in serum at every timepoint, while MK-7 (half-life ~3 days) was well absorbed and accumulated. MK-4's real evidence exists only at the ~10,000×-higher 45 mg pharmacological drug dose; the common low-dose MK-4 supplement has no pharmacokinetic rationale.

    5. No official body sets a vitamin-K2 intake, and "everyone is K2-deficient" is a marker-based overstatement.

    Both EFSA (70 µg/day) and the US IOM (120/90 µg/day) set intakes for phylloquinone (K1) ONLY, explicitly calling the menaquinone evidence insufficient. Frank (bleeding) deficiency is rare; extra-hepatic under-carboxylation is common but is a surrogate, not a deficiency disease.

    6. The body makes MK-4 from dietary K1 — so K2 is not as cleanly "separate and essential" as sold.

    The enzyme UBIAD1 synthesizes MK-4 endogenously from dietary K1 (via menadione), and isotope tracing confirms cerebral MK-4 comes from K1. Adequate K1 intake feeds the endogenous MK-4 route, undercutting the "you can only get K2 from a supplement" framing.

    7. The one real safety issue is the warfarin/VKA interaction — even a tiny MK-7 supplement lowers INR.

    In anticoagulated volunteers, MK-7 45 µg/day lowered INR ~40%, and even 10-20 µg/day mattered in 40-60% of subjects; MK-7's long half-life makes it more disruptive than dietary K1, and the authors concluded MK-7 supplements must be avoided on VKA therapy.

    8. Otherwise K2 is very safe — no upper limit and no target-organ toxicity even at the 45 mg drug dose.

    Neither the IOM nor EFSA sets a Tolerable Upper Intake Level for vitamin K because no toxic dose has been found; MK-4 45 mg/day has been used for years in Japan and high-dose MK-7 trials (up to ~1,080 µg) report only mild GI intolerance.

    9. Menadione (vitamin K3) is a different, genuinely toxic compound — not the same as MK-4/MK-7.

    Menadione is a synthetic provitamin that depletes glutathione and causes hemolytic anemia and kernicterus — it was withdrawn from human use — and its toxicity says nothing about the fully-substituted menaquinones MK-4/MK-7. The "vitamin K is dangerous" claims almost always confuse K3 with K2.

    Tier 2 — Real but modest, surrogate-level, or population-specific (9 findings)

    10. "K2 reverses arterial plaque" is refuted — no trial ever showed regression, and the pivotal independent RCT was flatly null.

    AVADEC (MK-7 720 µg + D, N=365, independent) found no difference in aortic-valve calcification despite crashing the biomarker (p=0.64); a diabetes PET trial trended the wrong way. Not one RCT reported net regression of existing calcium — the honest ceiling is "slower progression in some groups," never reversal.

    11. The best cardiovascular case is modest slowing of calcification in some groups — small, surrogate, and mixed.

    One newest positive coronary-calcium RCT (VitaK-CAC, MK-7 360 µg, slower CAC progression p=0.02) and a tiny meta-analytic effect (CAC −17 AU) sit against several nulls; arterial stiffness improved in one industry-funded trial's high-risk subgroup only. Real but weak, on surrogates, with no hard-outcome data.

    12. Bone: MK-7 slows density loss modestly and inconsistently — a surrogate, not a fracture, result.

    The flagship MK-7 trial slowed BMD decline at spine and femoral neck (not total hip) over 3 years, but it had no fracture endpoint; independent trials are weaker still. Moving bone density is not the same as preventing a break.

    13. K2 has no trustworthy fracture evidence outside a discredited Japanese high-dose MK-4 drug literature.

    The eye-catching fracture reductions come from old, single-country, largely open-label MK-4 45 mg trials, and the largest, most rigorous MK-4 fracture trial (the OF study, N=4,378) was NEGATIVE on its primary endpoint. The most current independent synthesis rates the evidence low-quality and cannot confirm an anti-fracture benefit.

    14. The famous K2-fracture data was partly built on research-misconduct retractions.

    Several trials in the fracture meta-analyses came from Yoshihiro Sato, subject to one of medicine's largest retraction records; re-running the analysis without them substantially attenuated the anti-fracture effect. The strongest "K2 prevents fractures" numbers do not survive a clean dataset.

    15. Independent Western K1 trials fix the biomarker and are null on bone density — the surrogate-vs-outcome gap in one line.

    ECKO (K1 5 mg) and Booth (K1 500 µg) both lowered undercarboxylated osteocalcin yet showed no BMD benefit at any site, echoed by Binkley. Vitamin K reliably corrects the lab number and reliably fails to move the bone.

    16. The observational K2 signal is real but confounded — and worse K status predicts death, not heart attacks.

    The Rotterdam Study linked high dietary menaquinone to lower CHD mortality (RR 0.43) and less aortic calcification, but K2 tracks cheese/dairy and broader lifestyle. Tellingly, in the Bruneck cohort poor K status predicted CVD and all-cause mortality (HR 1.40) but NOT myocardial infarction or sudden death — the opposite of the "keeps calcium out of your coronaries" story.

    17. In kidney/dialysis patients — K2's strongest rationale — every hard calcification endpoint is null.

    CKD/dialysis patients have the worst K status and most calcification, giving K2 its best mechanistic case, yet Trevasc-HDK, iPACK-HD, Valkyrie and VitaVasK all improved the biomarker and failed to slow real calcification or stiffness. iPACK-HD is the cleanest "fixing the marker ≠ fixing the artery" result.

    18. Functional vitamin-K insufficiency (by dp-ucMGP) is genuinely common — the honest kernel behind the marketing.

    A measurable slice of apparently healthy adults, and more of the elderly, carry elevated dp-ucMGP, meaning their extra-hepatic tissues are not maximally carboxylated even with normal clotting. That is a real, MK-7-correctable state — it is just not the same as a disease that supplementing has been shown to prevent.

    Tier 3 — Weak or unproven: marketing outruns the data (6 findings)

    19. The D3+K2 "synergy / K2 directs the calcium" thesis is mechanism-marketing.

    D induces the Gla-proteins and K activates them — biologically coherent — but no human RCT shows that adding K2 to D3 changes any hard outcome versus D3 alone, and there is no validated dose ratio. Same-meal timing is a fat-absorption nicety, not an outcome lever.

    20. "High-dose D3 alone calcifies your arteries" is unsupported in humans at normal doses.

    The WHI coronary-calcium substudy found no difference with calcium + D3; the scare is extrapolated from warfarin-plus-D rat models (where the K pathway is pharmacologically blocked) and a Medical Hypotheses paper, not human data. Genuine vitamin-D toxicity is a megadose problem, not a "you forgot your K2" problem.

    21. Diabetes/glucose: one 4-week n=42 trial plus a mouse mechanism that failed in knockouts.

    The osteocalcin-as-insulin-hormone rationale rests on a mouse model that two 2020 knockout strains failed to reproduce, and the only human RCT (MK-4, 4 weeks, 42 healthy young men) moved a surrogate, not disease; cohort associations are confounded and meta-analyses are weak. No RCT shows K2 prevents diabetes.

    22. Cancer: only observational intake associations — the one supplement test was overturned by a phase-III futility stop.

    Dietary menaquinone tracked lower prostate and total-cancer risk in one cohort, and a tiny open-label subgroup suggested less liver cancer — but a large double-blind phase-III RCT of MK-4 for liver-cancer recurrence was stopped at interim for futility. The supplement claim was tested and failed.

    23. Inflammation, osteoarthritis, exercise and COVID: null RCTs or marker-only associations.

    Vitamin-K status tracks lower CRP/IL-6 cross-sectionally but attenuates on adjustment; the hand-osteoarthritis RCT was null overall; the exercise "cardiac output" finding is a single tiny study; and in COVID a real marker association did not translate to a clinical benefit in the pilot RCT.

    24. dp-ucMGP predicts mortality — but that is a marker / reverse-causation signal, and no K2 mortality RCT exists.

    Poor K status (high dp-ucMGP) rises with age, illness and kidney dysfunction, so "low K status predicts death" is heavily confounded by frailty. No randomized trial has ever tested whether supplementing K2 reduces mortality.

    Tier 4 — Myths and refuted claims (6 findings)

    25. "K2 boosts testosterone" is refuted — rat-only, the mechanism runs backwards, and the osteocalcin-hormone story collapsed.

    The claim rests on a single high-dose study in rats and testis-tumor cells with no human data, and the osteocalcin→Leydig-cell bridge it invokes needs under-carboxylated osteocalcin — the exact form K2 removes by carboxylating it, so the logic is inverted. Independent knockout mice then showed osteocalcin is not required for testosterone synthesis at all.

    26. "You MUST pair K2 with vitamin D" is not an established necessity.

    Co-supplementing is cheap, safe and plausible, but no human evidence shows it is required to make D3 "safe" or to change any hard outcome. Reasonable to do; dishonest to sell as an obligation.

    27. "MK-4 is superior" / "MK-7 is superior" — each is half-right, and marketing exploits the gap.

    MK-7 is clearly superior for nutritional-dose absorption and once-daily dosing; MK-4's only real evidence is the 45 mg pharmacological drug dose, where tissue delivery not serum accumulation is the proposed mechanism. Neither is universally "best," and the two evidence bases must never be pooled.

    28. "K2 for dental health / Weston-Price 'Activator X'" has no human trial behind it.

    No controlled human caries trial has ever tested K2, and the historical "Activator X = vitamin K2" identification was published in a non-peer-reviewed foundation journal, not the scientific literature. Modern support is animal, cross-sectional or mechanistic only.

    29. EFSA authorized only nutrient-adequacy claims and REJECTED the K2 heart/vessel claim.

    The EU authorized that vitamin K "contributes to maintenance of normal bones" and "normal blood coagulation" — deficiency-avoidance claims, not therapeutic ones — and it REJECTED the vitamin-K2 "normal function of the heart and blood vessels" claim on reassessment. "K2 protects your arteries — EFSA approved" is spin.

    30. Most positive MK-7 literature is manufacturer-funded surrogate work; the independent hard-endpoint trials skew null.

    The recurring positive MK-7 studies (bone, stiffness) are NattoPharma/Gnosis/Kappa-linked, surrogate-endpoint, sometimes from the same cohort, while the independently-funded, hard-endpoint trials (AVADEC, K4Kidneys) are null. The funding/endpoint pattern is the whole story.

    Practical — what to actually do (5 findings)

    31. If you take it, MK-7 all-trans is the form — and product quality matters more than any toxicity concern.

    Only the all-trans MK-7 isomer is bioactive, yet an analysis of commercial products found most below label and one with no detectable all-trans at all; a modest dose (~90-180 µg) already maxes out biomarker carboxylation, with diminishing returns above that. Buy a third-party-verified all-trans product; don't megadose.

    32. Food beats a pill for K2 status — and natto is in a league of its own.

    Natto (fermented soy) is by far the richest MK-7 source; hard cheeses, egg yolk and liver supply far less, and gut-bacterial menaquinone is poorly absorbed. Outside natto-eating diets, habitual long-chain K2 intake is genuinely low.

    33. Warfarin users: never self-start K2 — but DOACs are unaffected.

    K2 antagonizes warfarin and lowers INR (raising clot risk), so it must not be started without an anticoagulation clinic; consistency of intake, not restriction, is what governs VKA stability. Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) act downstream of vitamin K and are not affected.

    34. High-dose vitamin E antagonizes vitamin K — relevant if you stack supplements.

    1,000 IU/day of vitamin E raised PIVKA-II (worsened functional K status) in adults with normal coagulation. Stacking high-dose E can blunt K function, which matters most for anyone on a VKA or with marginal K status.

    35. The honest reason to take K2: correct a functional K deficit or pair it with D3 on plausibility — not to de-calcify arteries, prevent fractures, or raise testosterone.

    K2 reliably corrects a real, measurable status marker and is safe and cheap, so pairing MK-7 with D3 is a defensible plausibility play — but on current evidence it will not reverse arterial calcification, prevent a fracture, or boost testosterone. Take it for what it does, not for the marketing.
