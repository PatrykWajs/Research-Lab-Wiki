# Alpha-GPC: What It Actually Does vs What It Is Sold For

> A prescription dementia drug sold as a nootropic, whose headline claims trace to one unpublished conference abstract, and whose brain effect has never been measured in a living human.

*Literature reviewed to 18 September 2026. 135 sources, every identifier resolved against PubMed.*

## Start here

!!! quote "Bottom line"
    **Alpha-GPC carries the same amount of choline as the cheapest powder on the shelf, delivers no more of it to your blood, and its effect on the human brain has never once been measured in a living person.**

**Myths vs. reality**

| What people believe | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Alpha-GPC is a concentrated, premium form of choline | It is **40.5% choline by weight**. Choline bitartrate, the cheap form, is **41.1%**. They are the same to within half a percentage point. |
| It is far better absorbed than other choline forms | Matched on choline content, plasma choline was **identical** across alpha-GPC, choline chloride, choline bitartrate and egg lecithin - and alpha-GPC was the **lowest** over the first six hours. |
| It beats citicoline, that is proven | That study was an **injection**, and the advantage **vanishes** once you adjust for the choline each compound contains. The regulator says so in as many words. |
| It crosses into the brain and raises acetylcholine | **No one has ever measured brain choline or acetylcholine in a living human after a dose.** The claim comes from rats given five to eleven times a human serving. |
| It boosts growth hormone 44-fold | That is **one seven-man conference abstract from 2008, never published**, whose two arms began ten-fold apart on baseline. The manufacturer's own 20-man replication found **nothing**. |
| It adds 14% to your bench press | Same seven-man poster. When the same lab ran it again with 20 people, the result was **p = 0.159** and all eight physical measures were null. |
| It is proven for memory in healthy people | Four trials, **139 people, over 33 years**. The one with a hard attention endpoint was **null** - while confirming the compound was absorbed. |
| More is better | In three separate datasets the **lower dose beat the higher one**, and the higher dose sometimes did worse than placebo. |
| "8% of people are choline deficient, so supplement" | That statistic counts people below an *Adequate Intake*, which the National Academies' own manual says **may not be used** that way. |

**What you actually learn**

- Why the choline percentage of a molecule settles more marketing claims than any trial does.
- How an "injection study" quietly becomes a claim about a capsule.
- What it means when the ingredient's own manufacturer funds the trial that fails to replicate its headline claim.
- Why every positive result in a literature coming from studies of 7 to 13 people, and every larger study being null, is a pattern rather than a coincidence.
- How to read a safety signal that is large, dose-responsive, independently funded, and still probably confounded.

!!! tip "What this means for you"
    - **If you want choline, eat two eggs.** A 600 mg capsule delivers 243 mg of choline; one egg carries about 147 mg.
    - **Do not buy it for a bigger lift.** No exercise result has ever been independently replicated, and no trial has ever measured muscle or fat.
    - **Do not buy it for growth hormone.** The claim is an unpublished abstract in seven men that the manufacturer's own trial could not reproduce.
    - **Know that you are taking a prescription drug at a non-prescription dose.** It is a licensed medicine in Italy, South Korea and Russia, dosed there at 1,200 mg a day.
    - **The serving size is above what regulators assessed.** Europe evaluated 203.7 mg a day; the US filing says 250 mg a day. A common serving is 600 mg.
    - **Treat the stroke question as open, not settled.** A 12-million-person cohort found more strokes in users; it is probably confounded, and nobody has resolved it in either direction.


!!! abstract "TL;DR"

    Alpha-GPC is **40.5% choline by weight**. Choline bitartrate, the cheapest form on the shelf, is **41.1%**.[^S78] Matched on choline, alpha-GPC puts **no more choline in your blood** than choline chloride, choline bitartrate or egg lecithin - and over the first six hours it was the lowest of the four.[^S14] The famous "beats citicoline" result was an **intramuscular injection** study, and its advantage **disappears** once you adjust for the choline each compound actually contains.[^S10][^S78]

    **Nobody has ever measured choline or acetylcholine inside a living human brain after a dose.** Not by spectroscopy, not by PET, not in spinal fluid, at any dose, by any route. The measurement is routine for endogenous brain choline.[^S8] The brain claim rests on three rat studies at **five to eleven times a human serving**, in which brain radioactivity came out **at or below blood levels**.[^S3]

    In healthy people the entire evidence base is **four trials, 139 participants, spread over 33 years**.[^S9][^S48][^S13][^S52] The one with a hard, pre-specified attention endpoint was **null** - while confirming the compound was absorbed.[^S48]

    The sports claims - "+44-fold growth hormone", "+14% bench press" - come from **one seven-man conference abstract from 2008 that was never published**, whose two arms began ten-fold apart on baseline. When the ingredient's own manufacturer funded a 20-man replication, growth hormone came back at **p = 0.438** and **all eight physical outcomes were null**.[^S52] **No trial has ever measured body composition.**

    In its real indication, Korea's regulator **ordered** a re-evaluation and attached a repayment clause. The 852-patient result **missed its primary endpoint**. Meanwhile a cohort of **12 million Koreans** found **43% more strokes** in users - contested, probably confounded, and never answered in print.[^S70]

!!! tip "The picture, ranked by how strongly the evidence agrees"

    - **Settled:** same choline density as the cheapest form; no absorption advantage at matched dose; zero human brain measurements; four small trials in healthy people; nothing in exercise ever independently replicated; no body-composition data of any kind.
    - **Real, with limits:** it is a genuine prescription medicine at 1,200 mg/day; choline itself is genuinely essential and deficiency does real damage.
    - **Failed when tested properly:** the state-ordered 852-patient dementia trial; the sponsor-funded growth hormone replication; the 48-person strength replication.
    - **Unresolved:** the stroke signal, which is one large confounded cohort against another pointing the opposite way.

## What this review covered

Nine independent evidence sweeps: chemistry and pharmacokinetics; cognition in healthy adults; dementia, mild impairment and stroke; exercise performance; hormones; the stroke safety cohort; the TMAO pathway; choline as a nutrient; and regulation, product quality and the market.

Every identifier was then resolved against the PubMed summary database and checked against the claim it was cited for. **135 resolved, none wrong, none missing, none retracted.** Funding was recorded per study, because the cognition literature and the sports literature in this field are funded by **different industries** and should never be described in one sentence.

One search caution shapes everything: **glycerophosphocholine is also a normal metabolite of membrane turnover**, so a naive query returns 1,600 to 2,500 records from metabolomics and brain-spectroscopy studies where nobody administered anything. Hand-screening leaves roughly **21 human studies in which alpha-GPC was actually given to a person**.

## Tier 1 - The strongest, settled evidence

**It is not a more concentrated choline source. It is the same as the cheap one.** The molecule weighs 257.22 g/mol and the choline part weighs 104.17, so it is **40.50% choline by weight** - a figure four independent sources reproduce exactly.[^S78][^S14][^S100] Choline bitartrate is 41.1%. Choline chloride is 74.6%, nearly twice as dense. A **600 mg serving delivers 243 mg of choline**.

**Matched on choline, it delivers no advantage.** A four-way crossover gave 550 mg of choline as chloride, bitartrate, alpha-GPC or egg lecithin. Plasma choline exposure over 24 hours: 235.2, 233.5, **244.9** and 244.3 µmol/L·h - no difference, and over the first six hours alpha-GPC was **numerically the lowest**.[^S14] A deuterium-labelled replication found the same, and showed the labelled choline is gone from blood within five hours.[^S16] Both trials are small (six people each), so they exclude a large advantage rather than a modest one. The funder makes infant formula and sells none of the four as a nootropic.

**The citicoline comparison dissolves on inspection.** Gatti 1992 gave 1,000 mg of each by **intramuscular injection** and reported roughly double the plasma choline for alpha-GPC.[^S10] The European regulator re-analysed the same data: normalised to the choline each compound delivered, "the difference between treatments was no longer statistically significant".[^S78] The 1.9-fold advantage is the ratio of their choline contents, and nothing else.

**It does not reach your blood as itself.** Every human study measures plasma choline, because alpha-GPC "is hydrolyzed in the gut mucosa to form free choline".[^S12] The intact molecule has never been measured in human blood. In quantity, **1,200 mg raises peak plasma choline about 35%** for a few hours, and the response is sub-proportional: 7.4 times the dose buys 2.5 times the peak.[^S12][^S100]

**The brain measurement has never been made.** No study has measured choline or acetylcholine in a living human brain after alpha-GPC - by spectroscopy, PET or spinal fluid, at any dose, by any route. Endogenous brain choline compounds are measured routinely in healthy volunteers.[^S8] Nobody has run the before-and-after experiment. The only human brain imaging inside an alpha-GPC trial measured the **size** of brain regions, not their chemistry.[^S31]

**The rat data argues against preferential brain delivery.** The radiolabel study found brain radioactivity **comparable to or half of** blood, no intact alpha-GPC in brain tissue, and most of the dose exhaled as carbon dioxide.[^S3] The studies that raised brain acetylcholine used 300 to 600 mg/kg,[^S2][^S1] which converts to roughly **3,400-6,800 mg for a 70 kg adult** - five to eleven times a capsule.

**In healthy people, the whole literature is four trials and 139 participants.** Canal 1991 (n=32),[^S9] Marcus 2017 (n=48),[^S48] Tamura 2021 (n=39)[^S13] and Kerksick 2024 (n=20).[^S52] None had more than 48 people, none was multi-site, and **none ran longer than two weeks**. The oldest of them is cited as proof of a nootropic effect but tested something else: whether alpha-GPC reverses amnesia caused by an injected drug that blocks the acetylcholine system.[^S9] Reversing a chemical blockade shows the compound is cholinergic. It says nothing about a person whose acetylcholine system has not been knocked down.

**The trial with the hardest endpoint was null.** Seven days of dosing, a validated attention test: "no significant differences by group (p > 0.5)" on reaction time, worst reaction time and lapses - while blood choline rose in both dose arms.[^S48] Absorption works. The effect does not follow.

**Nothing in exercise has ever been independently replicated.** Six indexed human trials, four testing alpha-GPC alone, 93 participants between them. **Every positive strength or power result comes from n = 7, 12 or 13. Every trial with n ≥ 20 is null on its primary outcome.**[^S46][^S48][^S52][^S49] The only outcome replicated twice is a null.

**No trial has ever measured muscle or fat.** Not a weak trial, not a null one - none exists. Body composition appears in these papers only as a baseline description. The longest exposure with any performance outcome is seven days.

## Tier 2 - Real, with limits

**This is a medicine, at a dose nobody sells you.** Alpha-GPC is prescription-only in Italy (as Gliatilin, and **not reimbursed** by the health service), in South Korea, and in Russia.[^S130] The pharmaceutical regimen is **400 mg three times daily**.[^S12] Essentially every trial worth citing used 1,200 mg/day.[^S69][^S25][^S71][^S42] Importing a dementia result into a 600 mg pre-workout scoop imports it at half the studied dose.

**Choline itself is genuinely essential, and the deficiency work is real.** Fed under 50 mg a day, **77% of men and 80% of postmenopausal women** developed fatty liver or muscle damage, which resolved when choline was added back.[^S113][^S109] Patients on intravenous nutrition given choline reversed their liver steatosis, which returned ten weeks after withdrawal.[^S108] Several common gene variants raise individual requirement sharply.[^S112] This is the real biology the supplement borrows its credibility from.

**But every one of those results comes from near-zero intake.** The depletion protocols ran at 13 to 50 mg a day. Average intake is around 390 mg a day for men. **No intermediate dose has ever been tested** - the National Academies say so in the document that sets the reference value. What cohorts of ordinary people show is a **U-shape**, with the lowest dementia risk at **333-354 mg/day** across 125,594 people, and the highest intake quartile doing **worse** than the second.[^S128][^S127] In 2,497 men followed 22 years, total choline intake had **no** association with dementia; only the form found in eggs and liver did.[^S122]

**A 600 mg serving is under two eggs.** One large egg carries about 147 mg of choline; a 3 oz portion of beef liver carries about 355 mg.[^S121] The capsule delivers 243 mg.

## Tier 3 - Failed when tested properly

**Korea forced a test, and the drug missed.** Choline alfoscerate entered Korean reimbursement in 2005 under a system that never assessed value, and grew into one of the country's largest drug expenditures.[^S130][^S133] In June 2020 the drug regulator ordered a full clinical re-evaluation; the health ministry cut coverage for the non-dementia indications, raising patient copayment from 30% to 80%; and the insurer wrote clauses requiring manufacturers to **repay 20% of prescription revenue** if the trials failed. The makers litigated for five years and lost at the Supreme Court in March 2025. The copayment change took effect on 21 September 2025, and prescriptions fell **29.5%** over the next two quarters.

The trials reported in mid-2026: **852 patients across two 48-week placebo-controlled trials** in mild cognitive impairment. **The primary endpoint was not met.** The sponsors are arguing from a per-protocol subgroup at p = 0.0482 - in trials where their own financial liability is a direct function of that number. *(This result is reported by Korean pharmaceutical trade press; no peer-reviewed publication exists, no registry results are posted, and all three trial records have been stale since 2021 or 2024.)* Two drugs ahead of it in the same Korean re-evaluation queue, acetyl-L-carnitine and oxiracetam, both failed and were withdrawn.

**The flagship dementia trial is unregistered and has never finished.** ASCOMALVA has six publications and one patient cohort. Its enrolment target of 210 was never reached, every published result is an interim analysis, and there is no final report 14 years after the first.[^S22][^S25][^S31][^S32] The acknowledgements thank a manufacturer for supplying the drug, the placebo and the researchers' bursaries, while the funding statement in the same articles reads "the authors have no funding to report".

**The meta-analysis behind the efficacy claim counts that one cohort four times.** It reports "four RCTs, 350 participants"; all four are ASCOMALVA interim reports of at most 113 patients, and its authors are ASCOMALVA's own investigators.[^S34] The one trial in its evidence table with a clean risk-of-bias profile - registered, quadruple-blinded, academically sponsored - appears in **none** of the pooled estimates. It is the null one.[^S71]

**That null trial is the only one of its kind.** CONIVaD, 62 patients, twelve months: no difference on the primary cognitive endpoint or any secondary endpoint, with 96% adherence to the study drug.[^S71] The largest positive monotherapy trial, by contrast, reports a cognitive separation roughly **twice** what donepezil and its class achieve, from a single listed author, unregistered, with an unresolved funder, never replicated in 23 years.[^S69]

**The stroke-recovery claim has no control group behind it.** The 2,044-patient Italian study everyone cites describes itself as "an open multicentre trial" with no comparator.[^S67] Every number in it is a change over six months, which is exactly when untreated stroke patients recover on their own. The meta-analysis pooling it concedes in its own methods that three of its five studies had no control group of any kind.[^S73] In the same paper, citicoline - which **was** tested against real placebos in 3,901 patients - failed. An independent 2025 network meta-analysis of drugs for post-stroke cognitive impairment includes **zero** alpha-GPC trials.[^S40]

**The strength replication failed in the same lab that ran the original.** Bellar 2015 reported a positive isometric result at p = 0.044, but the p values are **one-tailed**; recomputed two-tailed they give about p = 0.10 with a confidence interval crossing zero, and part of the gap is the placebo arm declining rather than the treatment arm rising.[^S46] The same lab's 48-person follow-up found no difference on that exact outcome - and the only arm that beat placebo on strength was **caffeine**.[^S48]

## Tier 4 - Refuted

**The growth hormone claim is a seven-man conference abstract that was never published.** It reports a "44-fold" rise. The two arms began **ten-fold apart on baseline** growth hormone (0.19 against 1.9 ng/mL) inside a crossover of seven people, so the fold-change is an artefact of that imbalance. In absolute terms the peaks were 8.4 against 5.0 ng/mL - a gap smaller than the placebo arm's own spread. The same poster reported **no effect** on peak power, rate of force development or metabolic rate.

**The sponsor's own replication found nothing.** Twenty men, both doses, a full post-exercise time course, funded by the maker of the ingredient tested: growth hormone AUC 380 against 414 against 351 for high dose, low dose and placebo, **p = 0.438**, with the placebo arm numerically highest at 30 and 60 minutes.[^S52] The only other positive study is eight men, where growth hormone rose at 60 minutes, sat **below placebo** at 120 minutes, and the authors themselves compare the peak to 30 minutes of easy cardio. Glycerol, the direct marker of fat release, did not move at all.[^S56]

**And an acute hormone spike would not matter anyway.** Training one arm in a high-hormone state and the other in a low-hormone state produces **identical** muscle growth.[^S54] Across 56 people, hormone exposure did not correlate with lean mass or strength gains.[^S57] Across 49 people, no blood hormone predicted growth.[^S60] The field's own review of the question is titled "Hormones, Hypertrophy, and Hype".[^S66]

**No alpha-GPC trial has ever measured testosterone, IGF-1 or cortisol.** Not one, and no registered trial lists any of them as an outcome. The only other hormone ever measured moved the wrong way: thyroid-stimulating hormone fell significantly in the 500 mg arm.[^S48]

**The "+14% bench press" figure is the same seven-man poster.** The implied effect exceeds what weeks of creatine loading produce. When the same contract research organisation ran the protocol again with 20 people, bench press peak force came back at **p = 0.159**, and all eight physical outcomes were null.[^S52]

**Taking more makes the numbers worse.** In three independent datasets the lower dose outperforms the higher, and the higher dose sometimes underperforms placebo.[^S48][^S52] No proposed mechanism reverses direction with dose.

**"A clinical trial of 2,000+ participants" does not exist.** The four-figure patient count in seller copy is a **narrative tabulation of 13 published trials**, not a trial.[^S68] The largest single placebo-controlled trial in Alzheimer's has 261 patients.[^S69]

**"Only 8% of people get enough choline" is a statistic the rulebook forbids.** It counts people below an *Adequate Intake*, and the National Academies' own manual states that prevalence of inadequacy **may not** be calculated that way, because it produces "potentially major overestimates".[^S120][^S121] The Adequate Intake itself is a rounded extrapolation from one study of 15 men, and the women's figure was never measured - it is a body-weight scaling from the men. The author who published the 8% figure is paid by a choline ingredient manufacturer, an egg trade group and a beef trade group - and published the null that undercuts it: in 1,438 Americans with genotype data, the lowest third of choline intake showed **no** elevation in liver markers, overall or by genotype.[^S129]

## The stroke question, which is genuinely unresolved

In 2021, a cohort of **12,008,977 Koreans** aged 50 and over reported a **43% higher adjusted hazard of stroke** in people prescribed alpha-GPC over a mean eight years.[^S70] In absolute terms, derived from its own event counts: 16.2 strokes per 1,000 person-years in users against 11.3 in non-users - about **four extra strokes per 100 users over eight years**. It was funded by the national insurance service and a government research foundation, with **no industry involvement**.

**Three features resist the obvious explanation.** Risk rose with duration of use *within the user group*, which no comparison to non-users can explain (1.13, 1.18, then 1.36 as prescriptions passed 2, 6 and 12 months). The hazard was **larger** in the younger and healthier strata. Adding health-behaviour covariates did not shrink it.

**Three features undermine it.** Users were seven years older and twice as likely to carry serious comorbidity, and the study matched on only four variables. The drug is prescribed to people with cognitive complaints, who already carry vascular risk. And the two published critiques making exactly that point are **reader comments on the journal's website, not peer-reviewed correspondence** - which the authors never answered.

**A second cohort in the same database points the other way** - 508,107 patients with mild cognitive impairment, reporting lower dementia conversion and lower stroke risk.[^S76] But its methods assign the ICD codes for ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke **backwards**, its own event counts confirm the labels are inverted, and its better-adjusted propensity-matched analysis found **no** stroke difference in either direction. The same database elsewhere produces a 20% lower death rate in users,[^S74] the classic fingerprint of healthy-adherer bias, and a clean null on kidney cancer from the stroke paper's own research group.[^S79]

The honest summary: **the more the analyses adjust, the smaller the effect gets, in both directions**. No randomised trial is remotely powered for a stroke endpoint, and the longest blinded exposure ever run is twelve months in 62 people.

### The mechanism proposed to explain it

Gut bacteria convert choline into a precursor of TMAO, a metabolite linked to clotting. The first two steps are established in humans.[^S82][^S105] For alpha-GPC specifically: **162 mg produced no change** over eight hours;[^S100] about 490 mg produced detectable labelled TMAO;[^S16] about 1,360 mg raised it clearly.[^S14] **The 300-600 mg range people actually buy has never been tested**, and the total human sample across all three trials is 23 people.

Two results cut against the alarm. The isotope tracer shows alpha-GPC produces about **2.6 times less** labelled TMAO than an equal amount of choline as a salt, and produces it seven hours later,[^S16] because a gut enzyme must cut the molecule before bacteria can reach the choline.[^S97] And whole eggs barely raise TMAO at all, while choline salts do.[^S92][^S90]

**Whether TMAO causes vascular events is unsettled.** Pooled cohorts report 1.6 to 1.9 times higher risk, but with heterogeneity near 94% - the studies barely agree with each other.[^S87][^S86] Genetic (Mendelian randomisation) analyses find **no causal effect**, and find the arrow running backwards: kidney disease and diabetes raise TMAO.[^S89][^S95] The largest clean prospective set, 7,834 people, found plasma **choline** predicted cardiovascular disease while TMAO did not.[^S96] And fish raises TMAO **46 to 62 times** more than eggs or beef,[^S84] yet tracks with slightly lower heart disease.[^S93] The mouse study showing alpha-GPC promotes atherosclerosis used **1% of the animals' entire diet for 16 weeks**.[^S72]

## Dose, regulation and what is in the bottle

| Jurisdiction | Status | Dose ceiling the regulator recognises |
|---|---|---|
| Italy | Prescription medicine (Gliatilin), not reimbursed | 400 mg capsules, 1,000 mg ampoules |
| South Korea | Prescription; reimbursement restricted since Sep 2025 | 400 mg three times daily |
| Russia | Prescription medicine | up to 1,000 mg injection |
| United States | Lawful dietary ingredient | **250 mg/day** as filed |
| European Union | Novel food, assessed but not yet authorised | **203.7 mg/day** |
| China | Approved new food raw material (2024) | ≤600 mg/day (reported) |

A typical **600 mg serving is about three times the level Europe assessed** and more than double what the FDA has on file.[^S78] The ingredient supplier's dossier argued for 700 mg/day; the condition of use actually filed is 250 mg/day. The consumer dose sits in a gap: above what was evaluated for supplement safety, below what was tested for efficacy.

On product quality the finding is a **gap**. There is no published independent multi-brand assay of alpha-GPC content in marketed supplements. A validated method exists for measuring the isomeric impurity in commercial products,[^S131] which implies the impurity is a real commercial risk, but no market survey using it has been published. The raw material is strongly hygroscopic - it turns sticky in humid air, which is why the original product dissolved the drug in glycerin inside a soft capsule.[^S12]

## Bottom line

Alpha-GPC is a real cholinergic compound and a real medicine. As a supplement, the claims fail one by one: it is not a denser choline source than the cheap form, it delivers no more choline to your blood at matched dose, nobody has measured what it does inside a human brain, the healthy-adult evidence is four small trials over 33 years, the growth hormone claim is an unpublished abstract the manufacturer's own trial could not reproduce, no exercise result has ever been independently replicated, and no trial has ever measured muscle or fat.

In its actual medical indication, the trial the state ordered missed its primary endpoint. And the largest safety dataset in existence found more strokes in users, in a way that eight years of analysis has neither confirmed nor cleared.

If the goal is choline, two eggs deliver the same amount for less money, with better evidence behind the food than the capsule.

??? note "Tier 1 — Settled (13 findings)"

    **1. Alpha-GPC is 40.5% choline by weight, and that is the same choline density as the cheapest form on the shelf.**

    The molecule is 257.22 g/mol, the choline cation is 104.17, so 104.17/257.22 = 40.50%. A 600 mg serving delivers **243 mg of choline**. Choline bitartrate is 41.1% choline; choline chloride is 74.6%. Four published sources reproduce the conversion exactly. Any claim that alpha-GPC is a more concentrated choline source than the cheap forms is arithmetically false.

    **2. Matched on choline content, alpha-GPC delivers no more choline to the blood than choline chloride, choline bitartrate or egg phosphatidylcholine.**

    A four-way crossover gave 550 mg of choline as each of the four compounds: plasma choline AUC over 24 hours was 244.9, 235.2, 233.5 and 244.3 µmol/L·h, with no difference between them. Over the first six hours alpha-GPC was numerically the **lowest** of the four. The funder makes infant formula and sells none of the four as a nootropic.

    **3. A deuterium-traced replication reached the same answer, and showed the label is cleared from blood within five hours.**

    Labelling the choline moiety removes the background-diet confound entirely. Peak labelled choline was 2.36 µmol/L with no significant difference across the water-soluble forms, and by five hours it had returned to zero.

    **4. The famous "alpha-GPC beats citicoline" result is a molecular-weight artefact, and it was an injection study.**

    Gatti 1992 gave 1,000 mg of each **intramuscularly** and reported roughly double the plasma choline for alpha-GPC. The European regulator re-analysed the same data: normalised to the choline actually delivered (405 mg vs 213 mg), "the difference between treatments was no longer statistically significant". The 1.90× ratio is the ratio of their choline contents and nothing else.

    **5. Nobody has ever measured choline or acetylcholine inside a living human brain after a dose of alpha-GPC.**

    Not by spectroscopy, not by PET, not in spinal fluid, at any dose, by any route. The measurement is feasible and is done routinely for endogenous brain choline. The only human brain imaging inside an alpha-GPC trial measured the **size** of brain regions, not their chemistry.

    **6. The rat data behind "it crosses the blood-brain barrier" shows brain levels at or below blood levels, and no intact alpha-GPC in the brain at all.**

    The radiolabel study reports brain radioactivity comparable to, or half of, blood; the brain metabolites are "small amounts of choline and two unknown metabolites"; and 59-86% of the dose leaves as exhaled carbon dioxide. What the rodent work supports is that alpha-GPC is broken down to choline in the gut and some of that choline reaches the brain like any other choline.

    **7. The rodent doses that raised brain acetylcholine are five to eleven times a human serving.**

    Peak effects came at 300 mg/kg and 600 mg/kg in rats. Converted by body surface area, that is roughly 3,400-6,800 mg for a 70 kg adult, against a 600 mg capsule.

    **8. In humans, 1,200 mg raises peak blood choline by about 35%, for a few hours.**

    The best oral study (n=48, bioequivalence design) gives a peak rise of about 3.7 µmol/L against a normal fasting level near 8-10 µmol/L. An independent 1,200 mg study 30 years earlier found the same 3.5 µmol/L rise. The dose-response is markedly **sub-proportional**: 162 mg gives +1.40 µmol/L, so a 7.4-fold dose increase buys a 2.5-fold larger peak.

    **9. The parent molecule has never been measured in human blood, because it does not survive the gut.**

    Every human study measures choline. The bioequivalence paper states it plainly: alpha-GPC "is hydrolyzed in the gut mucosa to form free choline", so choline is what gets assayed.

    **10. The entire case for alpha-GPC in healthy people is four small trials, 139 people, spread over 33 years.**

    Randomised placebo-controlled trials giving alpha-GPC alone and measuring any cognitive or mood outcome in people without impairment: Canal 1991 (n=32), Marcus 2017 (n=48), Tamura 2021 (n=39) and Kerksick 2024 (n=20). None had more than 48 participants, none was multi-site, and none ran longer than **two weeks**.

    **11. The one trial with a hard, objective attention measure was flatly null, while proving the compound was absorbed.**

    Seven days at 250 or 500 mg, psychomotor vigilance: "no significant differences by group (p > 0.5)" on mean reaction time, worst reaction time and lapses. Blood choline rose in both dose arms. Absorption is not the bottleneck. Effect is.

    **12. The newest positive trial was null on 11 of its 13 cognitive measures.**

    Kerksick 2024 reports two significant Stroop variables out of thirteen, with no correction for multiple comparisons, no sample-size calculation, and registration filed five months after the last participant. It was funded by the maker of the ingredient tested, and the author is a paid advisor to that sponsor.

    **13. Not one exercise result has ever been reproduced by an independent group.**

    Bench press force: positive in a 7-man poster, null twice since. Isometric mid-thigh pull: positive in one 13-man trial, null in the same lab's 48-man follow-up. Vertical jump: null in four of five tests. The only outcome replicated twice is a **null** (upper-body isometric strength).


??? note "Tier 2 — Real, with limits (12 findings)"

    **14. This is a prescription medicine in Italy, South Korea and Russia, and has been for decades.**

    In Italy it is Gliatilin, sold in 400 mg capsules and 1,000 mg ampoules, prescription-only and **not reimbursed** by the health service. Korea reimbursed it from 2005 under a system that never required proof of value. Russia lists it as prescription-only. The supplement version is the same molecule at a quarter to half the dose.

    **15. Every clinical trial worth citing used 1,200 mg a day, two to four times a typical supplement serving.**

    The pharmaceutical regimen is 400 mg three times daily. Importing a dementia-trial result into a 600 mg pre-workout scoop imports it at half the studied dose.

    **16. Korea ordered the manufacturers to prove it works. The trial missed its primary endpoint.**

    The drug regulator ordered a clinical re-evaluation in June 2020. Two placebo-controlled 48-week trials in mild cognitive impairment, 852 patients combined, run by the manufacturers' own 57-company consortium, reported to the regulator in mid-2026. The primary analysis failed; the sponsors are arguing from a per-protocol subgroup at p = 0.0482. *(This result is reported by Korean pharmaceutical trade press; no peer-reviewed publication and no registry results exist, and the trial records have not been updated since 2021.)*

    **17. The only registered, independently sponsored, placebo-controlled trial in impaired patients was null.**

    CONIVaD, 62 patients, twelve months, quadruple-blinded: no difference on the primary cognitive endpoint or on any secondary endpoint. Adherence to the study drug was 96%, so the null is not a compliance artefact.

    **18. The flagship Alzheimer's trial is unregistered, has never finished, and its drug came from a manufacturer.**

    ASCOMALVA has six publications and one patient cohort. Its enrolment target of 210 was never reached, every published result is an interim analysis, and no final report exists 14 years after the first. The acknowledgements thank MDM S.p.A. for supplying the drug, the placebo and the researchers' bursaries, while the funding statement in the same papers reads "the authors have no funding to report".

    **19. The meta-analysis that made the case counts the same patients up to four times.**

    It pools "four RCTs, 350 participants" for the add-on effect. All four are ASCOMALVA interim reports of one cohort of at most 113 people, and the authors of the meta-analysis are the trial's own investigators. The one trial in the set with a clean risk-of-bias profile, CONIVaD, appears in the tables but in none of the pooled estimates. It is the null one.

    **20. The largest positive trial reports an effect twice the size of approved Alzheimer's drugs, and has never been replicated in 23 years.**

    De Jesus Moreno 2003, 261 patients, 180 days, single listed author, no registration, funder unresolved. The recomputed separation is about 6 points on the ADAS-Cog scale, against 2-3 points for donepezil and its class.

    **21. The two recent placebo-controlled trials in mild impairment are small, short and manufacturer-funded.**

    Jeon 2024: 600 mg for 12 weeks, p = 0.048, funded by the capsule's maker with a co-author at the manufacturer's research institute, and 26 of 100 randomised patients missing from the analysis. Sohn 2025: 36 people, funded by a Korean manufacturer, with the six-month look non-significant.

    **22. The stroke-recovery claim rests on a trial with no control group.**

    The 2,044-patient Italian study everyone cites is explicitly "an open multicentre trial" with no comparator. Every number in it is a change over six months, which is exactly the window in which untreated stroke patients recover on their own.

    **23. The meta-analysis of alpha-GPC in stroke says the quiet part in its own methods.**

    Three of its five alpha-GPC studies "had no control group with either a placebo or other active drugs", so the pooled "mean difference" is a before-and-after change inside a single arm. In the same paper, citicoline, which **was** tested against real placebos in 3,901 patients, failed.

    **24. An independent 2025 network meta-analysis of drugs for post-stroke cognitive impairment contains zero alpha-GPC trials.**

    Sixteen studies, 5,599 participants, comparing seven drugs. No alpha-GPC trial cleared the inclusion bar in the drug's own headline indication.

    **25. Choline itself is genuinely essential, and running people out of it does real damage.**

    Fed under 50 mg a day, 77% of men and 80% of postmenopausal women developed fatty liver or muscle damage, which resolved when choline was added back. Total parenteral nutrition patients given choline reversed their liver steatosis, and it returned ten weeks after withdrawal. This is the real biology the supplement borrows its credibility from.

    **26. A cohort of 12 million Koreans found 43% higher stroke risk in users, with no industry money anywhere near it.**

    Adjusted hazard ratio 1.43 for total stroke over a mean eight years, 1.34 ischaemic and 1.37 haemorrhagic, against matched non-users. Funded by the national insurance service and a government research foundation, conflicts of interest: none reported.

    **27. In absolute terms that is about four extra strokes per 100 users over eight years.**

    16.2 strokes per 1,000 person-years in users against 11.3 in non-users, a difference of 4.9 per 1,000 person-years, or roughly one extra stroke per 204 person-years of use. Derived from the study's own event counts.

    **28. Three features of that study resist the obvious "sicker people get prescribed it" explanation.**

    Risk rose with duration of use inside the user group alone (1.13, 1.18, then 1.36 as prescriptions passed 2, 6 and 12 months); the hazard was **larger** in the younger and healthier strata, not the sickest; and adding smoking and health-behaviour covariates did not shrink it.

    **29. It is still probably confounded, and the criticism was never answered in print.**

    Users were seven years older and twice as likely to carry serious comorbidity, and the study matched on only four variables. Two critiques exist, both arguing that people prescribed a cognitive drug already carry vascular disease. **Both are website comments on the journal page, not peer-reviewed correspondence**, and the authors never replied to either.

    **30. A second cohort in the same national database points the opposite way, and its stroke codes are swapped.**

    508,107 patients with mild cognitive impairment: lower dementia conversion, and stroke risk reported as lower, not higher. But its methods assign ICD code I61 to ischaemic stroke and I63 to haemorrhagic, which is backwards, and its own event counts confirm the labels are inverted. Its propensity-matched sensitivity analysis, the one adjusting for smoking, alcohol and exercise, found **no** stroke difference in either direction.

    **31. The same database also produced a 20% lower death rate and a clean null on kidney cancer.**

    Lower all-cause mortality in dementia patients using the drug is the classic signature of healthy-adherer bias, not a benefit. The kidney cancer null came from the same research group as the stroke paper, using a richer matching design. Read together, these say the database cannot cleanly separate the drug from the patient.

    **32. Alpha-GPC does raise TMAO, a gut-bacterial metabolite linked to clotting, but only at doses above a typical serving.**

    162 mg produced no change at all over eight hours. About 490 mg produced detectable labelled TMAO, and about 1,360 mg raised it clearly. **The 300-600 mg range that people actually buy has never been tested.** Twenty-three people in total have ever had this measured.

    **33. Against the popular framing, alpha-GPC generates less TMAO than a plain choline salt, and later.**

    The isotope tracer gives 0.20 µmol/L·h for alpha-GPC against 0.52 for an equimolar choline salt, peaking roughly seven hours later. The reason is mechanical: a host gut enzyme has to cut the molecule before bacteria can reach the choline.

    **34. Whether TMAO causes heart attacks at all is unsettled, and the genetic evidence says no.**

    Pooled cohorts report 1.6 to 1.9 times higher mortality with high TMAO, but with heterogeneity near 94%, meaning the studies disagree almost completely. Mendelian randomisation finds no causal effect, and finds the arrow running backwards: kidney disease and diabetes **raise** TMAO. The largest clean prospective set, 7,834 people, found choline predicted heart disease and TMAO did not. Fish raises TMAO 46-62 times more than eggs or beef and tracks with lower heart risk.

    **35. The mouse study showing alpha-GPC causes atherosclerosis used a dose in the region of fifteen times a human serving.**

    One percent of the animals' entire diet for 16 weeks, producing TMAO levels 14-18 times control and clear artery lesions. The genuinely useful part of that paper is the human bridge: human stool bacteria do convert alpha-GPC to the TMAO precursor in a dish.


??? note "Tier 4 — Refuted (9 findings)"

    **36. The growth hormone claim is a 7-man conference abstract from 2008 that was never published.**

    It reports a "44-fold" rise. The two arms started ten-fold apart on baseline growth hormone (0.19 against 1.9 ng/mL) in a crossover of seven people, so the fold-change is a baseline artefact. In absolute terms the peaks were 8.4 against 5.0 ng/mL, a gap smaller than the placebo arm's own spread. The same poster found **no** effect on peak power, rate of force development or metabolic rate.

    **37. The sponsor's own 20-man trial failed to replicate it, at both doses, on every time point.**

    Same squat protocol, full time course, growth hormone AUC 380 against 414 against 351 for high dose, low dose and placebo, p = 0.438. At 30 and 60 minutes the **placebo** arm was numerically highest.

    **38. The only other positive hormone study is eight men, and the effect equals one easy cardio session.**

    Growth hormone rose at 60 minutes and was **lower than placebo** by 120 minutes; the authors themselves compare the size to 30 minutes of moderate exercise. Glycerol, the direct marker of fat release, did not move at all.

    **39. Acute hormone spikes do not build muscle, whatever causes them.**

    Training the same person's arms in a high-hormone versus low-hormone state produced identical muscle growth; across 56 people, hormone exposure did not correlate with lean mass or strength gains; in 49 people, no blood hormone predicted growth. This is a settled question in exercise physiology.

    **40. No alpha-GPC trial has ever measured testosterone, IGF-1 or cortisol, and the one other hormone measured went down.**

    Zero trials, and zero registered trials, list any of the three as an outcome. Thyroid-stimulating hormone fell significantly in the 500 mg arm of the seven-day trial.

    **41. The "+14% bench press" figure is the same 7-man poster, and the lab that ran it could not reproduce it.**

    The effect size implied is larger than weeks of creatine loading produce. When the same contract research organisation ran the protocol again with 20 people, bench press peak force came out at p = 0.159, and all eight physical outcomes were null.

    **42. The second-most-cited positive result is significant only on a one-tailed test.**

    Recomputing from the published statistics, the two-tailed p is about 0.10 and the confidence interval for the difference crosses zero. Part of the "effect" is the placebo arm declining rather than the treatment arm rising, and the reported analysis does not match the analysis the methods describe.

    **43. Doubling the dose makes the results worse in three separate datasets.**

    500 mg was worse than placebo where 250 mg was better; 400 mg was worse than 200 mg; 630 mg was numerically worse than placebo on three of eight outcomes. No mechanism explains a compound that reverses direction when you take more of it.

    **44. "A clinical trial of 2,000+ participants" does not exist, and "only 8% of people get enough choline" is a statistic the rulebook forbids.**

    The four-figure patient count sellers cite is a narrative tabulation of 13 published trials, not a trial. And the deficiency figure counts people below an Adequate Intake, which the National Academies' own manual says "may not be used" as a cut-point because it produces "potentially major overestimates" of inadequacy. The author who published it is paid by a choline ingredient maker and an egg trade group, and his own analysis of 1,438 Americans found the lowest third of choline intake showed **no** elevation in liver markers, overall or by genotype.
