# Zinc: What It Actually Does vs What People Think

> An essential mineral whose real benefits are almost entirely deficiency-correction — where the one settled, life-saving use is the one nobody markets, the winter-immunity and testosterone claims mostly don't survive, and "harmless" turns out to be the biggest myth of all.

*Independent research, 24 August 2026. 82 cited sources (76 PubMed-verified — every PMID title-matched against PubMed, 0 wrong). Not medical advice.*

## Start here

!!! quote "Bottom line"
    **Zinc is essential, and correcting a genuine deficiency does real good — but topping up a well-fed adult mostly doesn't. And "harmless" is the biggest myth: chronic dosing above 40 mg/day causes copper deficiency and, at extremes, an often-irreversible nerve disease. Status predicts health; a pill mostly helps if you're actually low.**

**What you actually learn**

- **Zinc's one settled, life-saving use is the one nobody markets** — treating diarrhea and preventing pneumonia in deficient children (WHO standard-of-care).
- **Almost every adult benefit is deficiency-correction** — testosterone, immunity, glycemia and taste all move only when you're actually low; topping up a replete person does little.
- **Serum zinc is a poor test** — it barely tracks intake and falls during any illness, so "low zinc predicts disease" is mostly reverse causation.
- **The consumer claims mostly fail:** no cold prevention, no testosterone in replete men, nothing for COVID, no fertility benefit, no "detox."
- **"Harmless" is false:** copper depletion is the real harm, the 40 mg/day limit is built on it, and nasal zinc can destroy your sense of smell.

!!! tip "What this means for you"
    - **Eat zinc before you supplement it** — oysters, red meat and shellfish carry the most absorbable zinc; plant sources count but phytate lowers their availability.
    - **Supplement only for a real reason:** a plausible dietary gap or a deficiency (strict vegan/high-phytate diet, elderly, heavy alcohol, malabsorption, pregnancy) — not testosterone or "winter immunity."
    - **If you do:** a modest dose (~15-25 mg), citrate or gluconate is fine, with a little food if it upsets your stomach; stay under 40 mg/day and pair copper if you dose chronically high.
    - **For a cold,** if anything: zinc-*acetate* lozenges, started in the first 24 hours, no citric-acid/mannitol binders — and don't keep megadosing.
    - **Don't take it for** testosterone, "immune boosting," or "heavy-metal detox," and never put zinc up your nose.

## What this review covered

Zinc is the winter-immunity aisle's flagship mineral and a fixture of "testosterone booster" stacks. The claim load: everyone's deficient, it cures colds, it boosts immunity and testosterone, it detoxes heavy metals, the "chelated/picolinate" form is far superior, and it's harmless. A seven-cluster evidence sweep — foundations, forms, immunity/colds, testosterone/fertility, skin/metabolic/eye/mood, safety, and the marketing myths — tested each claim against the peer-reviewed literature: Cochrane reviews, randomized trials, Mendelian-randomization studies and large cohorts. Every one of the 76 PubMed citations was title-matched against PubMed before use (0 wrong). The shape that emerges is the same family as the [vitamin D](vitamin-d.md) and [magnesium](magnesium.md) stories — deficiency-correction, not boosting — with zinc's own twists.

## Tier 1 — The strongest evidence

**Zinc is genuinely essential, and the body keeps no reserve of it.** Zinc is catalytic or structural in about 10% of the human proteome — some 2,800 proteins and over 300 enzymes[^S1]. Unlike iron or calcium, there is **no dedicated zinc store and no storage hormone**; the body defends blood zinc by tuning absorption and gut excretion[^S2]. That has a load-bearing consequence: **serum zinc is a poor individual marker.** In a 4,347-person analysis it was not related to how much zinc people ate or supplemented — it tracked sex, age, fasting and time of day[^S3]. Zinc is also a *negative acute-phase reactant* (it drops during any inflammation), so "low zinc predicts disease" studies are heavily confounded by reverse causation; proper status uses intake plus adjusted plasma zinc plus growth, never plasma zinc alone[^S4].

**"Everyone is deficient / the soil is depleted" is refuted for the well-fed adult — but real globally.** True biochemical deficiency in the developed world is uncommon, and the "modern soil is empty" story is mostly a yield-dilution and analytical-method artifact[^S8][^S9]. Globally, though, ~17% of people are at risk of inadequate intake (a wide 12-66% band), concentrated in high-phytate, low-animal-food diets[^S5][^S6], and specific developed-world groups have a genuine gap — vegans, the elderly, alcoholics, malabsorption, pregnancy[^S7]. It is a **targeted, not universal, shortfall.**

**The strongest, life-saving evidence is childhood diarrhea therapy — the part nobody markets.** In deficient children, oral zinc shortens diarrhea and cuts recurrence, which is why WHO/UNICEF recommend it for every childhood diarrhea episode[^S10][^S11] (a large trial found 5 mg works as well as 10-20 mg with less vomiting[^S12]); zinc also reduces childhood pneumonia in deficient populations, at low certainty[^S13]. This is genuine, guideline-level, mortality-relevant medicine — and it is deficiency-population therapy, not a template for an adult with a sniffle.

**Copper depletion is the dose-limiter — and the real harm the "harmless" story ignores.** Fifty mg/day of zinc for ten weeks cut a copper-status enzyme by 47%[^S14], which is exactly why the Tolerable Upper Intake Level is **40 mg/day**, set on copper, not acute toxicity[^S15]. Chronic excess (100-300 mg/day, or a hidden source like zinc-laden denture cream) causes acquired copper deficiency presenting as anemia, neutropenia and a **myeloneuropathy that mimics B12 degeneration and frequently does not reverse**[^S16][^S17][^S18]. Separately, **intranasal zinc causes permanent anosmia** — the 2009 FDA warning to stop using Zicam nasal gel — a route-specific harm that does not apply to swallowed zinc[^S19][^S20]. Zinc is also a real **drug**: zinc acetate has been FDA-approved since 1997 as maintenance therapy for Wilson's disease[^S21].

**"Advanced" zinc forms mostly don't beat cheap zinc.** The gold-standard stable-isotope study found citrate ≈ gluconate (~61% absorbed) >> oxide (~50%)[^S22]. "Picolinate is best" rests on a single unreplicated 1987 study with weak markers and industry authors[^S23]; bisglycinate has one small acute-dose serum spike (n=12) and no proven retention benefit[^S24]; and a "glycoprotein-matrix" product only beat the worst form, oxide, in a manufacturer-funded trial[^S25].

## Tier 2 — Real but bounded (deficiency-gated or narrow)

**Testosterone is the archetypal deficiency-vs-replete trap.** Experimental zinc restriction collapses testosterone (young men 39.9 → 10.6 nmol/L) and repletion roughly doubles it in marginally-deficient elderly men[^S26]; a controlled ward study confirms induced deficiency lowers it[^S27]. But a 38-study systematic review is explicit that the effect **depends on baseline status — little to none when zinc is already adequate**[^S28], and the direct test in zinc-*sufficient* men found a high-dose supplement raised blood zinc while leaving testosterone unchanged[^S29]. **ZMA** — the bodybuilding staple — is a clean null: the independent trial found no effect on testosterone, IGF-1, cortisol or strength[^S30], and the only positive study was co-authored and marketed by the ZMA patent-holder[^S31]. **Fertility** was settled by the largest trial in the space: FAZST (N=2,370) found 30 mg zinc + folic acid for six months did not improve live birth (34% vs 34%) or any semen parameter, with *more* side effects[^S32]; earlier positive trials measured sperm counts, not babies[^S33], and any benefit is confined to genuinely deficient/low-testosterone men[^S34]. And "zinc lowers DHT / stops hair loss" is an *in-vitro* story[^S35] that reverses in the one human dataset, where oral zinc made DHT go **up**[^S34].

**Colds: lozenges may shorten one, but zinc does not prevent them.** The earlier "zinc shortens colds by a day" Cochrane review was **withdrawn in 2015** for data errors and plagiarism[^S37]. The current 2024 Cochrane says zinc *may* cut cold duration ~2 days at **low certainty** and shows "little or no" preventive effect[^S36], while Hemilä's meta-analyses of zinc-*acetate lozenges* report a bigger ~40% effect[^S38][^S39] and argue Cochrane wrongly pooled tablets with lozenges[^S40]. Both camps analyse the same small trial pool[^S41]. The honest read: zinc-acetate lozenges started within 24 hours, at ~80-100 mg/day, releasing free ionic zinc (no citric-acid or mannitol binders that neutralize it), *probably* shorten an active cold — a real but soft, format-specific effect[^S42] — while "take zinc every winter to avoid colds" is unsupported.

**The other narrow wins.** Correcting deficiency in the elderly reduces infection (a small RCT[^S43]; a nursing-home cohort that is observational and frailty-confounded[^S44]). Oral zinc reduces inflammatory **acne** but is inferior to minocycline[^S50][^S51]. Zinc (with copper) is a real ingredient of the **AREDS** eye formula that slows progression to *advanced* macular degeneration in people who already have it — 25 mg as effective as the original 80 mg — not "eye health," not prevention[^S52][^S53][^S54]. And zinc lowers blood sugar modestly, largest where baseline zinc is low; a Mendelian-randomization triangulation found it causally lowers fasting glucose but not HbA1c[^S55][^S56]. Wound healing "in everyone" is refuted — it helps only if you're deficient[^S45].

## Tier 3 — Weak, unproven, or over-sold

Taste disorders respond mainly when they're due to deficiency or are idiopathic[^S57]. Depression's link to low serum zinc is largely reverse causation (zinc falls in illness), and supplementation is a weak, adjunct-only signal[^S58][^S59]. Hair-loss benefit is confined to the genuinely deficient[^S60]. Cortisol and thyroid effects are null-to-weak in replete people (thyroid only via deficiency-correction). And the athlete "zinc protects testosterone" signal is a small, single-lab study about blunting a training dip, not raising baseline testosterone.

## Tier 4 — Myths & marketing

**"More zinc = stronger immunity" is backwards above adequacy.** Correcting deficiency restores immune function, but there's no evidence pushing zinc higher makes a replete immune system "stronger," and **≥150 mg/day impairs immune cell function** and lowers HDL cholesterol[^S62][^S63]. **The COVID zinc-ionophore protocol failed its randomized test:** zinc inhibits coronavirus polymerase *in a dish* with an ionophore[^S46], but the COVID A to Z trial was stopped for futility[^S47], an RCT meta-analysis was null[^S48], and hydroxychloroquine isn't even a real zinc ionophore[^S49]. **"Zinc detoxes heavy metals"** has no human clinical evidence. **High-dose zinc carries a prostate-cancer signal, not protection:** supplemental zinc >100 mg/day tracked advanced prostate cancer in a 47,000-man cohort[^S64] (observational, unreplicated). **Tinnitus** is a clean Cochrane negative[^S61]. And "EFSA-approved" claims (immune, testosterone-maintenance, fertility) are *role / deficiency-avoidance* claims — not "boosting" claims — while thyroid, fatigue, muscle, heart, prostate and joint claims were rejected outright[^S65].

## Who actually benefits

The people for whom zinc genuinely earns its keep are specific: **deficient children** (diarrhea/pneumonia therapy), **people with a real dietary gap or deficiency** (strict vegan/high-phytate diets, the elderly, heavy alcohol use, malabsorption/bariatric surgery, pregnancy), **Wilson's-disease patients** (as a drug), and people with **intermediate/advanced AMD** (as the specific AREDS combination). For a well-fed omnivore who eats red meat and shellfish, the marginal benefit of a zinc pill for immunity or testosterone is close to zero — and above 40 mg/day it turns negative.

## The bottom line

Zinc is an essential mineral whose real benefits are almost entirely **deficiency-correction**, and its one settled, life-saving use — treating diarrhea in deficient children — is the one no supplement markets. Eat zinc before you supplement it: oysters, red meat and shellfish carry the most absorbable zinc (plant sources count but phytate lowers their availability). If you supplement, keep it modest (~15-25 mg, under the 40 mg/day limit), citrate or gluconate is fine (skip "picolinate/chelated" superiority), take it with a little food if it upsets your stomach, pair copper if you dose chronically high, and space it from tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics. The honest reason to take zinc is a plausible dietary gap or a real deficiency — **not** testosterone, immunity, or "detox."

## The full findings — all 35

Every conclusion this review reached, grouped by how strongly the evidence backs it. Each finding is distilled from the fully-cited evidence above.

??? note "Tier 1 — Settled: strongest, cleanest evidence (9 findings)"

    **1. Zinc is genuinely essential, and the body keeps no reserve of it.**

    Zinc is catalytic or structural in roughly **10% of the human proteome** (~2,800 proteins, >300 enzymes) and runs on ZIP/ZnT transporters buffered by metallothionein. Unlike iron or calcium there is **no dedicated zinc store and no storage hormone** — homeostasis is maintained by adjusting absorption and intestinal excretion, so intake matters continuously.

    **2. Serum zinc is a poor individual status marker — which quietly distorts the whole field.**

    In NHANES (n=4,347) serum zinc was **not associated with dietary or supplemental zinc intake**; it tracked sex, age, fasting and time-of-day instead. Zinc is also a **negative acute-phase reactant** (it falls during any inflammation/infection), so "low serum zinc predicts disease" studies are heavily confounded by **reverse causation** — the illness lowers the zinc, not the reverse. Proper status assessment combines intake + adjusted plasma zinc + growth, never plasma zinc alone.

    **3. The strongest, mortality-relevant zinc evidence is childhood diarrhea therapy — the part nobody markets.**

    WHO/UNICEF recommend **zinc 10–20 mg/day for 10–14 days for every childhood diarrhea episode**; Cochrane confirms it shortens diarrhea (more in the malnourished) and prevents recurrence, and a large trial found **5 mg works as well as 10–20 mg with less vomiting**. This deficiency-population therapy is zinc's one settled, guideline-level, life-saving use — the opposite of the "immune-booster" it's sold as in rich countries.

    **4. "Everyone is deficient / soil depletion" is refuted for the well-fed adult, but real globally.**

    True biochemical zinc deficiency in the developed world is uncommon (~4–8% low serum), and the "modern soil is depleted" story is mostly a yield-dilution and analytical-method artifact, not empty soil. Globally, though, **~17% of people are at risk** (range 12–66%), concentrated in high-phytate/low-animal-food diets, and specific developed-world groups have a genuine gap — vegans, the elderly, alcoholics, malabsorption, pregnancy. It is a **targeted, not universal, shortfall.**

    **5. Copper depletion is the dose-limiter — the 40 mg upper limit is literally built on it.**

    Zinc induces intestinal metallothionein, which traps dietary copper and sheds it in stool. Fifty mg/day for 10 weeks cut a copper-status enzyme (erythrocyte Cu-Zn-SOD) by **47%**, and the US Tolerable Upper Intake Level of **40 mg/day** is set on exactly this copper endpoint, not acute toxicity.

    **6. Chronic megadosing causes copper-deficiency myeloneuropathy — the real harm the "harmless" story ignores.**

    Sustained high-dose zinc (often 100–300 mg/day, or a hidden source) causes acquired copper deficiency presenting as a **myeloneuropathy that mimics B12 subacute combined degeneration, plus anemia/neutropenia** — and the neurologic damage frequently does **not reverse** on copper repletion. **Zinc-containing denture cream** (17,000–34,000 µg/g) is a classic stealth source that drove the Fixodent/Poligrip litigation.

    **7. Intranasal zinc causes permanent anosmia — but oral zinc does not.**

    Zinc salts applied to the nose are directly toxic to olfactory neurons; case series of lasting smell loss from intranasal zinc gluconate (Zicam) drove a **2009 FDA warning to stop using it**. This is a route-specific harm — swallowed zinc does not cause anosmia — and is worth separating cleanly from the oral-supplement question.

    **8. Zinc is a real approved drug — for Wilson's disease.**

    Zinc acetate (Galzin) has been **FDA-approved since 1997 as maintenance therapy for Wilson's disease**, using the same copper-blocking mechanism that makes it dangerous in excess for everyone else. This is zinc-as-pharmacology, distinct from any wellness claim.

    **9. "Advanced" zinc forms mostly don't beat cheap zinc.**

    The gold-standard stable-isotope study found **zinc citrate ≈ gluconate (~61% absorbed) >> oxide (~50%)**. "Picolinate is best" rests on a **single unreplicated 1987 study** with weak markers and industry authors; bisglycinate has one small acute-dose serum spike (n=12) and no proven retention/outcome benefit; and a "glycoprotein-matrix" product only beat the worst form (oxide) in a manufacturer-funded trial. Paying a premium for "chelated/advanced" zinc is not justified.


??? note "Tier 2 — Real but modest, and deficiency-gated (11 findings)"

    **10. Testosterone: zinc restores it in deficient men — that's the whole real effect.**

    Experimental zinc restriction dropped young men's testosterone (39.9→10.6 nmol/L) and repletion roughly doubled it in marginally-deficient elderly men; a controlled ward-depletion study confirmed induced deficiency lowers T. A 38-study systematic review is explicit: the effect **depends on baseline status — little to none when zinc is already adequate**.

    **11. In already-replete men, zinc does NOT raise testosterone — the "booster" claim is refuted.**

    In zinc-sufficient exercising men, a high-dose zinc supplement raised serum and urinary zinc (proving absorption) but produced **no change in total or free testosterone**. Raising a blood level is not the same as changing physiology; the marketing collapses the deficiency-vs-replete distinction on purpose.

    **12. ZMA is a clean null, propped up by one conflicted study.**

    The independent ZMA RCT (N=42 trained men) found **no effect on testosterone, IGF-1, cortisol or strength**, echoed by the Koehler null. The only positive ZMA-raises-T trial was **co-authored and marketed by the ZMA patent-holder** (Victor Conte, later of BALCO), in a non-indexed journal, never replicated.

    **13. Fertility: the definitive trial killed the "zinc improves sperm" claim.**

    FAZST (N=2,370) found 30 mg zinc + folic acid for 6 months did **not improve live birth (34% vs 34%) or any semen parameter**, with more GI side effects. Earlier positive trials measured sperm counts, not babies, and any residual benefit is confined to genuinely zinc-deficient/low-testosterone men; Cochrane calls the antioxidant/zinc fertility evidence very-low-certainty and formula-driven.

    **14. Cold lozenges may modestly shorten an active cold — if you catch it early and dose the right form.**

    Zinc-acetate lozenges started within 24 hours, at ~80–100 mg/day, releasing free ionic zinc (no citric-acid/mannitol binders), plausibly shorten a cold — Hemilä's meta-analyses say ~2.7 days/~40%, the 2024 Cochrane says ~2 days at **low certainty**, and the two camps argue over the same small trial pool. The mechanism is local ionic zinc in the throat, and formulation is decisive. **Probable but soft; the marketed "cuts your cold in half" oversells low-certainty data.**

    **15. Zinc does NOT reliably prevent colds.**

    Cochrane found "little or no" preventive effect on catching a cold, and a large respiratory-infection meta found the prevention signal weak and null for inoculated rhinovirus. Taking zinc "every winter to avoid colds" is unsupported — and chronic dosing above 40 mg/day risks copper deficiency.

    **16. Childhood pneumonia prevention is probable — in deficient populations, at low certainty.**

    Cochrane found zinc reduces pneumonia incidence in young children in high-deficiency settings, overall **low certainty**. Like the diarrhea result, this is deficiency-population therapy and does not transfer to a well-fed adult taking zinc for a sniffle.

    **17. Correcting deficiency in the elderly cuts infection — not "boosting" a replete immune system.**

    A small RCT (N=50, zinc 45 mg/day) found fewer infections and lower inflammatory markers in older adults, and a nursing-home cohort linked reaching normal serum zinc to ~50% less pneumonia — but that is **observational and confounded by frailty**. The signal supports deficiency-correction, not megadosing the already-replete.

    **18. Acne: a real but modest effect, weaker than antibiotics.**

    Meta-analysis shows oral zinc reduces inflammatory acne papules, but a head-to-head RCT found it **inferior to minocycline**. Zinc is a legitimate second-line or antibiotic-sparing option (limited by GI upset at effective doses), not an equal to tetracyclines — one of its better-evidenced non-deficiency uses.

    **19. AMD: zinc is a real ingredient of a specific eye formula — not "eye health" in general.**

    In AREDS (N=3,640), zinc (80 mg + copper) slowed progression to **advanced** age-related macular degeneration (zinc-alone OR 0.75; combo OR 0.72); AREDS2 showed **25 mg works as well as 80 mg**. This is a specific high-dose combination for people who **already have intermediate/advanced AMD** — not prevention, not general "eye vitamins," and it needs co-formulated copper.

    **20. Glycemia: a modest, mostly-in-diabetics effect — causal for fasting glucose only.**

    Meta-analyses show zinc lowers fasting glucose (~−20 mg/dL) and HbA1c (~−0.4%), largest where baseline zinc is low; a triangulation study with Mendelian randomization found zinc **causally lowers fasting glucose but has no causal effect on HbA1c, HOMA-IR or insulin**. Real but modest, and not a reason for a replete non-diabetic to supplement.


??? note "Tier 3 — Weak or unproven: marketing outruns the data (7 findings)"

    **21. Taste disorders: mostly a deficiency/idiopathic fix.**

    Meta-analysis found zinc improves taste disorders (RR 1.38), and an RCT helped idiopathic dysgeusia — but the benefit concentrates where zinc status is low or the disorder is idiopathic/renal, not as a universal taste fix.

    **22. Depression: low zinc is a marker, not a proven treatment.**

    Depressed patients have modestly lower serum zinc (−1.85 µmol/L), but zinc is a negative acute-phase reactant so this is heavily reverse-causation. Supplementation has only weak, mostly **adjunctive** (add-on to antidepressants) support from small heterogeneous trials.

    **23. Hair loss: only if you're actually deficient.**

    The "zinc for thicker hair" narrative rests on case series in zinc-deficient telogen effluvium and cross-sectional low-serum-zinc associations that are partly a stress marker. There is no good evidence zinc grows hair in zinc-replete people.

    **24. "Zinc lowers DHT / stops hair loss / treats the prostate" is an in-vitro story that reverses in humans.**

    Zinc's effect on prostatic 5-alpha-reductase is biphasic **in a test tube**, and the one human dataset that measured DHT after oral zinc found **DHT went UP, not down**. No controlled human trial shows oral zinc lowers DHT, shrinks the prostate, or stops hair loss.

    **25. Cortisol and thyroid: null-to-weak in replete people.**

    The best-controlled data show **no effect of zinc on cortisol** in trained men; zinc is genuinely required for thyroid-hormone metabolism, so correcting a deficiency can normalize thyroid markers, but that is deficiency-correction, not a "thyroid booster" for euthyroid replete people.

    **26. The athlete "zinc protects testosterone" signal is thin.**

    A small single-lab study found zinc blunted exercise-induced testosterone/thyroid suppression in wrestlers — i.e. protecting against a training dip in men with high zinc losses, not raising baseline T in a rested, replete man.

    **27. Wound healing "in everyone" is refuted.**

    Cochrane found no evidence oral zinc heals arterial/venous leg ulcers except in the subgroup with low baseline zinc. Zinc supports wound healing only when it corrects a deficiency.


??? note "Tier 4 — Myths and refuted claims (6 findings)"

    **28. "More zinc = stronger immunity" is backwards above adequacy.**

    Zinc is required for normal immune function, and correcting deficiency restores it — but there is no evidence pushing zinc above adequacy makes a replete immune system "stronger," and **high-dose zinc (≥150 mg/day) impairs lymphocyte and neutrophil function** and lowers HDL. The EFSA-authorised immune claim is a *role* claim, not a "boost" claim.

    **29. The COVID zinc-ionophore protocol failed its randomized test.**

    Zinc inhibits coronavirus polymerase **in a dish** with an ionophore, but the COVID A to Z RCT was **stopped for futility** (zinc did not shorten COVID), an RCT meta-analysis was null for mortality, the launching study was an unblinded retrospective, and hydroxychloroquine isn't even a real zinc ionophore. Refuted as a clinical protocol.

    **30. "Zinc detoxes / chelates heavy metals" has no human evidence.**

    The metallothionein/heavy-metal mechanism is real in animals and test tubes, but there is **no clinical-trial evidence** that zinc supplements remove lead, cadmium or mercury from people, and no approved use for it; genuine heavy-metal poisoning is treated with prescription chelators under supervision.

    **31. High-dose zinc carries an advanced-prostate-cancer signal — the opposite of "prostate protection."**

    In the Health Professionals Follow-Up Study (N=46,974), supplemental zinc **>100 mg/day was linked to advanced prostate cancer** (RR 2.29; ≥10 years' use RR 2.37). It's observational and unreplicated, so not proof of cause — but it is a harm signal, not the "zinc protects the prostate" claim marketing implies.

    **32. Tinnitus: a clean negative.**

    Cochrane found no evidence zinc supplementation improves adult tinnitus.

    **33. EFSA-"approved" zinc claims are role/deficiency claims — not "boosting" claims.**

    The authorised EU claims ("zinc contributes to normal immune function / cognitive function / fertility / **maintenance of normal serum testosterone** …") certify only that **adequate zinc is needed to keep those functions normal** — deficiency-avoidance, not that supplementing a replete person improves them. And EFSA **rejected** claims for thyroid, tiredness/fatigue, muscle, heart, prostate and joints. "Clinically proven to boost testosterone/immunity — EFSA approved" is marketing spin.


??? note "Practical — what to actually do (2 findings)"

    **34. Food first — and animal foods win on bioavailability.**

    Oysters, red meat and shellfish deliver the most absorbable zinc; plant sources (legumes, pumpkin/hemp/sesame seeds, whole grains) carry meaningful zinc but with lower bioavailability because **phytate** binds it — soaking, sprouting and fermentation help. Animal-food share is the strongest predictor of population zinc adequacy.

    **35. If you supplement, keep it modest and know why.**

    Take a modest elemental dose (~15–25 mg, stay under the 40 mg/day upper limit), citrate or gluconate is fine (skip "exotic" forms), with a little food if it upsets your stomach; if you dose chronically above ~40 mg **pair it with copper** and don't; and **space it from tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics** (it chelates them). The honest reason to take zinc is a plausible dietary gap or a real deficiency — not testosterone, immunity, or "detox."
