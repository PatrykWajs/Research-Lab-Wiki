# Vitamin K2 (MK-7): What It Actually Does vs What People Think

> A genuine cofactor that reliably fixes a status biomarker but has never been shown to fix a hard outcome — the clearest "the lab number moves, the disease doesn't" case in the supplement world, and weaker on outcomes than even vitamin D.

*Independent research, 30 August 2026. 85 cited sources (81 PubMed-verified — every PMID title-matched against PubMed; 1 wrong caught and corrected). Not medical advice.*

## Start here

!!! quote "Bottom line"

**What you actually learn**

- **The biomarker moves, the disease doesn't.** K2 drops dp-ucMGP 40-50% in every trial — and the calcification, fracture and event endpoints stay null. It is the purest "fixing the lab number isn't fixing you" case in the supplement aisle.
- **"Reverses plaque" is refuted.** Not one trial reported regression; the independent AVADEC aortic-valve trial was null, and no heart-attack or mortality trial exists.
- **The fracture case collapsed.** The eye-catching numbers came from a Japanese 45 mg *drug* dose, and removing the research-misconduct-tainted trials dissolved the effect; independent Western trials fix the marker and are null on bone.
- **The D3 pairing is plausible, not proven.** "K2 directs the calcium" and "D3 alone calcifies your arteries" are mechanism-marketing, not human evidence.
- **Very safe, with one real exception** — the warfarin interaction. And menadione (K3), the genuinely toxic form, is not the same thing as MK-4/MK-7.

!!! tip "What this means for you"
    - **Food beats a pill for K2** — natto (fermented soy) is by far the richest source; cheese, egg yolk and liver supply less.
    - **If you supplement:** all-trans MK-7, a modest dose (~90-180 µg) already maxes out the biomarker, taken with a fatty meal (ideally alongside D3). Product quality matters far more than any toxicity worry — much commercial MK-7 tests below its label.
    - **Pair it with D3 if you like** — cheap, safe, plausible — but expect a corrected biomarker, not de-calcified arteries or stronger bones.
    - **Don't take it for** reversing plaque, preventing fractures, or boosting testosterone — the evidence isn't there.
    - **On warfarin?** Never self-start K2 — it lowers your INR and raises clot risk. Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) are unaffected.

## What this review covered

Vitamin K2 (menaquinone) is sold as the missing cofactor that "keeps calcium out of your arteries and puts it in your bones," a mandatory partner for vitamin D3, and a booster for bone, heart and even testosterone. A seven-cluster evidence sweep — biochemistry and forms, bone, cardiovascular, the D3+K2 pairing, kidney/diabetes/other outcomes, safety and warfarin, and the marketing myths — tested each claim against the peer-reviewed literature: the pivotal randomized trials, the meta-analyses, the large cohorts, and the regulatory positions. Every PubMed citation was title-matched against PubMed before use, and one wrong agent-supplied identifier was caught and corrected. The shape that emerges is the same family as the [vitamin D](vitamin-d.md), [magnesium](magnesium.md) and [omega-3](omega-3-fish-oil.md) stories — a real biomarker that supplements move, and outcomes that don't follow — taken here to its purest extreme.

## Tier 1 — The strongest evidence

**The biochemistry is settled and genuinely elegant.** Vitamin K is the cofactor for the enzyme that activates the "Gla-proteins" — the clotting factors (its classical, life-or-death role), osteocalcin in bone, and Matrix Gla Protein (MGP) in the vessel wall; the recycling enzyme it depends on, VKOR, is exactly what warfarin blocks[^S1]. MGP is the most potent natural inhibitor of vascular calcification known: mice engineered without it calcify all their arteries and die of aortic rupture within about two months[^S28], and warfarin — which inactivates MGP — causes arterial calcification in rats and in humans[^S29][^S31]. This is why the *hypothesis* that K2 protects arteries is legitimate. The problem is everything downstream of the mechanism.

**K2 reliably fixes the vitamin-K status biomarker — that part is not in doubt.** Across bone, vascular, kidney and even COVID trials, MK-7 supplementation drives the functional markers (dephospho-uncarboxylated MGP, undercarboxylated osteocalcin) down by roughly 40-50% every single time[^S23][^S35][^S41][^S79]. Target engagement is the one thing the literature does consistently.

**But fixing the biomarker has not fixed the outcome — and that is the whole story.** The pivotal, largest, independent trial — AVADEC, which gave men with aortic-valve calcification MK-7 720 µg plus vitamin D for two years — crushed the biomarker and found **no difference in calcification progression** (p=0.64)[^S41]. The same "marker moves, disease doesn't" split repeats in dialysis (Trevasc-HDK, iPACK-HD) and in chronic-kidney-disease stiffness (K4Kidneys)[^S51][^S52][^S36]. This is the textbook surrogate-versus-outcome disconnect.

**MK-4 is not MK-7 — and low-dose MK-4 is a waste.** At a 420 µg nutritional dose, MK-4 (half-life about one hour) is **undetectable in the blood at every timepoint**, while MK-7 (half-life about three days) is well absorbed and accumulates[^S3][^S2][^S9]. MK-4's only real evidence exists at the 45 mg *pharmacological* drug dose — roughly 250-500 times a supplement — so a common low-dose MK-4 supplement has no pharmacokinetic rationale, and the two evidence bases must never be pooled. A further nuance: the body makes MK-4 endogenously from dietary K1 via the enzyme UBIAD1[^S4][^S5], so K2's status as "essential and separate from K1" is softer than marketed.

**No official body sets a K2 intake, and "everyone is deficient" is a marker-based overstatement.** Both EFSA (Adequate Intake 70 µg/day) and the US Institute of Medicine (120/90 µg/day) set values for phylloquinone (K1) *only*, explicitly calling the menaquinone evidence insufficient, and neither sets an upper limit[^S6][^S7]. Frank, bleeding deficiency is rare; extra-hepatic under-carboxylation is common but is a surrogate, not a disease.

**Safe — with one hard exception.** No Tolerable Upper Intake Level exists for vitamin K because no toxic dose has ever been found; MK-4 45 mg/day has been used for years in Japan and high-dose MK-7 trials report only mild stomach upset[^S6][^S77]. The exception is the **warfarin / vitamin-K-antagonist interaction**: because these drugs work by blocking vitamin-K recycling, even a 45 µg MK-7 supplement measurably lowers INR and raises clot risk, and MK-7's long half-life makes it more disruptive than dietary K1[^S73][^S2]. A warfarin patient must never self-start K2. Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) act downstream of vitamin K and are unaffected[^S10]. And **menadione (vitamin K3)** — a synthetic compound that depletes glutathione and causes hemolysis, withdrawn from human use — is a *different* substance whose toxicity says nothing about MK-4/MK-7[^S82]; the newborn "vitamin K shot" is K1, not K2[^S81].

## Tier 2 — Real but modest, surrogate-level, or population-specific

**"K2 reverses arterial plaque" is refuted.** Not a single randomized trial has reported net regression of existing arterial or valve calcium. AVADEC was null on aortic valve[^S41]; a diabetes trial using an ¹⁸F-fluoride PET scan actually trended the *wrong* way[^S40]. The honest ceiling is "slower progression in some groups": the newest coronary-calcium trial (VitaK-CAC, MK-7 360 µg, 180 patients) found slower calcium progression over two years[^S38], and a 2023 meta-analysis found a tiny pooled effect (−17 calcium units)[^S43] — but the AVADEC coronary substudy was null on its primary endpoint[^S39], and arterial stiffness improved in only one industry-funded trial's high-risk subgroup[^S35] against two independent nulls[^S36][^S37]. A systematic review concluded vitamin K does not consistently prevent calcification, stiffness or atherosclerosis[^S42].

**No cardiovascular event or mortality trial exists.** Every K2 vascular RCT is powered for an imaging or stiffness surrogate. The only event-level signal is an underpowered, exploratory composite in one substudy[^S39] — not enough to support any efficacy claim. The observational signal that seeded the whole field — the Rotterdam Study's finding that high dietary menaquinone tracked lower coronary-disease mortality[^S32] — is confounded (K2 comes from cheese and dairy, which track diet and lifestyle), and tellingly, in the Bruneck cohort poor K status predicted death but **not** heart attacks or sudden cardiac death[^S34], the opposite of what a "keeps calcium out of your coronaries" story predicts. This is the same biomarker-predicts-death-but-the-supplement-doesn't-fix-it pattern seen with omega-3, vitamin D and magnesium.

**Bone: the density moves modestly, the fracture data collapses.** Vitamin K reliably lowers undercarboxylated osteocalcin (the bone K marker) — but osteocalcin-knockout mice have *denser* bone, so that marker is a hypothesis, not a proven fracture pathway. At nutritional MK-7 doses, the flagship trial slowed density loss at spine and femoral neck (not total hip) over three years, with no fracture endpoint and manufacturer funding[^S23]. The famous fracture reductions come almost entirely from Japanese MK-4 45 mg drug-dose trials, most open-label[^S14], and two things demolish them: the **largest, most rigorous MK-4 fracture trial (the "OF study," 4,378 women) was NEGATIVE on its primary endpoint**[^S16], and several trials in the pooled meta-analyses came from a researcher subject to one of medicine's largest research-misconduct records — **re-running the analysis without the discredited trials dissolved the anti-fracture effect**[^S18][^S19]. The most current independent synthesis rates the evidence low-quality and cannot confirm a fracture benefit[^S21]. Independent Western trials using K1 (ECKO, Booth) fix the biomarker and are **null on bone density**[^S24][^S25]. No osteoporosis guideline outside Japan recommends vitamin K.

**Functional insufficiency is genuinely common — the honest kernel.** A measurable slice of apparently healthy adults, and more of the elderly, carry elevated dp-ucMGP, meaning their extra-hepatic tissues are not maximally carboxylated even with normal clotting[^S34][^S79]. That is a real, MK-7-correctable state. It is just not the same as a disease that supplementing has been shown to prevent. And in kidney/dialysis patients — where the deficit and rationale are strongest — every *hard* calcification endpoint is still null[^S51][^S52][^S37].

## Tier 3 — Weak or unproven: marketing outruns the data

**The D3+K2 "synergy" is mechanism-marketing.** The enzymology is real — vitamin D induces the Gla-proteins and K activates them — but no human trial shows that adding K2 to D3 changes any hard outcome versus D3 alone, and there is no validated dose ratio[^S44][^S48]. The review most cited as proof is a narrative review that itself hedges. Same-meal timing is a fat-absorption nicety, not an outcome lever.

**"High-dose D3 alone calcifies your arteries" is unsupported in humans.** The Women's Health Initiative coronary-calcium substudy found no difference with calcium plus D3[^S50]; the scare is extrapolated from warfarin-plus-vitamin-D **rat** models (where the K pathway is pharmacologically blocked)[^S45] and a *Medical Hypotheses* paper[^S46]. Genuine vitamin-D toxicity is a megadose problem, not a "you forgot your K2" problem.

**Diabetes, cancer, and the rest are marker associations, not supplement proof.** The osteocalcin-as-insulin-hormone rationale rests on a mouse model[^S56] that two 2020 knockout strains failed to reproduce[^S57], and the only human K2/glucose trial was four weeks in 42 healthy young men[^S60]. In cancer, observational menaquinone associations (prostate, lung)[^S65] and a tiny open-label liver-cancer subgroup[^S67] were **overturned by a large phase-III trial stopped for futility**[^S68]. Inflammation, osteoarthritis, exercise and COVID are cross-sectional or single-study signals that null out in the actual trials[^S63][^S69][^S72]. And while poor K status (dp-ucMGP) predicts mortality across cohorts[^S34], that marker rises with age, illness and kidney dysfunction — heavy reverse causation — and no K2 mortality trial exists.

## Tier 4 — Myths and refuted claims

**"K2 boosts testosterone" is refuted, and instructively so.** The claim traces to a single study in *rats* and testis-tumor cells at a very high dose, with no human data[^S83]. Worse, the mechanism runs **backwards**: the osteocalcin-to-Leydig-cell bridge it invokes needs *under*-carboxylated osteocalcin — the exact form K2 *removes* by carboxylating it. And the whole "osteocalcin is a hormone" edifice collapsed when independent labs built new knockout mice with **normal testosterone, glucose and muscle**[^S58][^S57]. There is no human evidence K2 raises testosterone.

**"You must pair K2 with vitamin D" is not an established necessity** — reasonable on plausibility, dishonest as a rule[^S44][^S50]. **"The advanced form is far superior"** is half-marketing: MK-7 wins for nutritional absorption, but MK-4's only real evidence is the 45 mg drug dose, and the two must not be conflated[^S3][^S16]. **"K2 cures cavities / Weston-Price's Activator X"** has no controlled human caries trial, and the "Activator X = K2" identification was never peer-reviewed. **"EFSA-approved for your heart"** is spin: EFSA authorized only the nutrient-adequacy claims that vitamin K contributes to "maintenance of normal bones" and "normal blood coagulation"[^S84], and it **rejected** the vitamin-K2 "normal function of the heart and blood vessels" claim on reassessment[^S85]. Finally, the **funding pattern** is the tell: the recurring positive MK-7 studies are manufacturer-linked, surrogate-endpoint and sometimes from the same cohort[^S35][^S23], while the independently funded, hard-endpoint trials skew null[^S41][^S36].

## Bottom line

Vitamin K2 is a real cofactor with a coherent mechanism and a genuinely correctable status marker, wrapped in claims its own trials don't support. It **reliably fixes dephospho-uncarboxylated MGP** and just as reliably **fails to move the hard endpoint** — no reversal of arterial calcification, no trustworthy fracture prevention, no testosterone effect, no proven diabetes, cancer or mortality benefit, and no evidence that pairing it with D3 changes any outcome. Its safety is excellent bar the warfarin interaction, its best supplement form is all-trans MK-7 (not low-dose MK-4), and its positive literature skews manufacturer-funded and surrogate-level while the independent hard-endpoint trials skew null. If you take it, take it for what it does — correct a real biomarker and add a cheap, plausible vascular hedge — not for the marketing.

MK-4's real evidence exists only at the ~10,000×-higher 45 mg pharmacological drug dose; the common low-dose MK-4 supplement has no pharmacokinetic rationale.

    Both EFSA (70 µg/day) and the US IOM (120/90 µg/day) set intakes for phylloquinone (K1) ONLY, explicitly calling the menaquinone evidence insufficient. Frank (bleeding) deficiency is rare; extra-hepatic under-carboxylation is common but is a surrogate, not a deficiency disease.

    **6. The body makes MK-4 from dietary K1 — so K2 is not as cleanly "separate and essential" as sold.**

    The enzyme UBIAD1 synthesizes MK-4 endogenously from dietary K1 (via menadione), and isotope tracing confirms cerebral MK-4 comes from K1. Adequate K1 intake feeds the endogenous MK-4 route, undercutting the "you can only get K2 from a supplement" framing.

    **7. The one real safety issue is the warfarin/VKA interaction — even a tiny MK-7 supplement lowers INR.**

    In anticoagulated volunteers, MK-7 45 µg/day lowered INR ~40%, and even 10-20 µg/day mattered in 40-60% of subjects; MK-7's long half-life makes it more disruptive than dietary K1, and the authors concluded MK-7 supplements must be avoided on VKA therapy.

    **8. Otherwise K2 is very safe — no upper limit and no target-organ toxicity even at the 45 mg drug dose.**

    Neither the IOM nor EFSA sets a Tolerable Upper Intake Level for vitamin K because no toxic dose has been found; MK-4 45 mg/day has been used for years in Japan and high-dose MK-7 trials (up to ~1,080 µg) report only mild GI intolerance.

    **9. Menadione (vitamin K3) is a different, genuinely toxic compound — not the same as MK-4/MK-7.**

    Menadione is a synthetic provitamin that depletes glutathione and causes hemolytic anemia and kernicterus — it was withdrawn from human use — and its toxicity says nothing about the fully-substituted menaquinones MK-4/MK-7. The "vitamin K is dangerous" claims almost always confuse K3 with K2.

    **25. "K2 boosts testosterone" is refuted — rat-only, the mechanism runs backwards, and the osteocalcin-hormone story collapsed.**

    The claim rests on a single high-dose study in rats and testis-tumor cells with no human data, and the osteocalcin→Leydig-cell bridge it invokes needs *under*-carboxylated osteocalcin — the exact form K2 *removes* by carboxylating it, so the logic is inverted.     No controlled human caries trial has ever tested K2, and the historical "Activator X = vitamin K2" identification was published in a non-peer-reviewed foundation journal, not the scientific literature. Modern support is animal, cross-sectional or mechanistic only.

    **29. EFSA authorized only nutrient-adequacy claims and REJECTED the K2 heart/vessel claim.**

    The EU authorized that vitamin K "contributes to maintenance of normal bones" and "normal blood coagulation" — deficiency-avoidance claims, not therapeutic ones — and it REJECTED the vitamin-K2 "normal function of the heart and blood vessels" claim on reassessment. "K2 protects your arteries — EFSA approved" is spin.

    **30. Most positive MK-7 literature is manufacturer-funded surrogate work; the independent hard-endpoint trials skew null.**

    The recurring positive MK-7 studies (bone, stiffness) are NattoPharma/Gnosis/Kappa-linked, surrogate-endpoint, sometimes from the same cohort, while the independently-funded, hard-endpoint trials (AVADEC, K4Kidneys) are null. The funding/endpoint pattern is the whole story.


??? note "Practical — what to actually do (5 findings)"

    **31. If you take it, MK-7 all-trans is the form — and product quality matters more than any toxicity concern.**

    Only the all-trans MK-7 isomer is bioactive, yet an analysis of commercial products found most below label and one with no detectable all-trans at all; a modest dose (~90-180 µg) already maxes out biomarker carboxylation, with diminishing returns above that. Buy a third-party-verified all-trans product; don't megadose.

    **32. Food beats a pill for K2 status — and natto is in a league of its own.**

    Natto (fermented soy) is by far the richest MK-7 source; hard cheeses, egg yolk and liver supply far less, and gut-bacterial menaquinone is poorly absorbed. Outside natto-eating diets, habitual long-chain K2 intake is genuinely low.

    **33. Warfarin users: never self-start K2 — but DOACs are unaffected.**

    K2 antagonizes warfarin and lowers INR (raising clot risk), so it must not be started without an anticoagulation clinic; consistency of intake, not restriction, is what governs VKA stability. Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) act downstream of vitamin K and are not affected.

    **34. High-dose vitamin E antagonizes vitamin K — relevant if you stack supplements.**

    1,000 IU/day of vitamin E raised PIVKA-II (worsened functional K status) in adults with normal coagulation. Stacking high-dose E can blunt K function, which matters most for anyone on a VKA or with marginal K status.

    **35. The honest reason to take K2: correct a functional K deficit or pair it with D3 on plausibility — not to de-calcify arteries, prevent fractures, or raise testosterone.**

    K2 reliably corrects a real, measurable status marker and is safe and cheap, so pairing MK-7 with D3 is a defensible plausibility play — but on current evidence it will not reverse arterial calcification, prevent a fracture, or boost testosterone. Take it for what it does, not for the marketing.

The observational source of the whole 'K2 protects arteries' story — confounded by diet.

Bruneck Study (N=684, 15.5 yr): worse K status (higher dp-ucMGP) predicted CVD (HR 1.23) and all-cause mortality (HR 1.40) — but NOT myocardial infarction or sudden cardiac death, the opposite of the 'keeps calcium out of coronaries' story. Classic biomarker-predicts-death pattern.
[^S35]: Knapen MHJ et al. 2015, *Thromb Haemost*. [PMID 25694037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25694037/). MK-7 180 µg/day, 3 yr, N=244: improved carotid-femoral pulse-wave velocity / arterial stiffness, mainly in the high-baseline-stiffness subgroup; dp-ucMGP fell ~50%. The single positive stiffness RCT — NattoPharma-funded, surrogate, subgroup-driven.
[^S36]: Witham MD et al. 2020, *J Am Soc Nephrol*. [PMID 32817311](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32817311/). K4Kidneys (MK-7 400 µg/day, 12 mo, N=159 CKD 3b-4): NO effect on aortic pulse-wave velocity or any vascular marker. Independent, well-designed, NULL.
[^S37]: De Vriese AS et al. 2020, *J Am Soc Nephrol*. [PMID 31704740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31704740/). Valkyrie (hemodialysis + AF, 18 mo): replacing warfarin ± MK-7 2000 µg 3x/wk improved K status but produced NO difference in coronary/aortic/valve calcium or PWV. Reversing the warfarin insult did not de-calcify.
[^S38]: Vossen LM et al. 2026, *JAMA Cardiol*. [PMID 42268593](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42268593/). VitaK-CAC (MK-7 360 µg/day, 2 yr, N=180 symptomatic CAD): CAC progression significantly slower with MK-7 (p=0.02). The newest — and one of the only — positive coronary-calcium RCTs; small, surrogate, no hard outcomes, possible manufacturer product support.
[^S39]: Hasific S et al. 2023, *JACC Adv*. [PMID 38938724](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38938724/). AVADEC coronary substudy (MK-7 720 µg + D, 2 yr, men): CAC progression NOT significantly different overall (p=0.089); a subgroup (CAC≥400) and an exploratory safety composite were hypothesis-generating only. Independent (Novo Nordisk Foundation).
[^S40]: Zwakenberg SR et al. 2019, *Am J Clin Nutr*. [PMID 31387121](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31387121/). MK-7 360 µg/day, 6 mo, N=68 T2D+CVD: femoral calcification activity on 18F-NaF PET tended to INCREASE (p=0.06) — null/paradoxical — while dp-ucMGP fell as expected.
[^S41]: Diederichsen ACP et al. 2022, *Circulation*. [PMID 35465686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35465686/). AVADEC (MK-7 720 µg + D3 1000 IU, 24 mo, N=365 men, aortic-valve calcium >300): PRIMARY aortic-valve-calcification progression NO difference (p=0.64) despite dp-ucMGP crashing (−212 vs +45). The largest, most rigorous, INDEPENDENT trial — biomarker moved, disease didn't.
[^S42]: Vlasschaert C et al. 2020, *Nutrients*. [PMID 32977548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32977548/). Systematic review: vitamin K does NOT consistently prevent progression of calcification, atherosclerosis or arterial stiffness; role 'uncertain'. Surrogates only.
[^S43]: Li T, Wang Y, Tu WP 2023, *Front Nutr*. [PMID 37252246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37252246/). Meta-analysis (14 RCTs, 1,533): pooled effect on CAC statistically significant but TINY (MD −17.37 AU, p=0.04); dp-ucMGP strongly reduced (MD −243). No hard-outcome meta possible.
[^S44]: van Ballegooijen AJ et al. 2017, *Int J Endocrinol*. [PMID 29138634](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29138634/). The narrative review most cited as proving 'D+K synergy'; read directly it concludes the mechanism is plausible but the joint-supplementation CV evidence is scarce and 'might' help — a hedge, not a demonstration.
[^S45]: Price PA et al. 2000, *Arterioscler Thromb Vasc Biol*. [PMID 10669626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10669626/). In rats, warfarin-induced artery calcification was ACCELERATED by vitamin D — the specific animal data behind 'high D is dangerous without K'. Note the K pathway is pharmacologically BLOCKED, not a normal-diet condition.
[^S46]: Masterjohn C 2007, *Med Hypotheses*. [PMID 17145139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17145139/). 'Vitamin D toxicity redefined' — proposes D toxicity is partly an induced vitamin-K deficiency, so K could mitigate it. Explicitly a HYPOTHESIS paper; the source of most online 'you MUST pair D3 with K2' content.
[^S48]: Kuang X et al. 2020, *Food Funct*. [PMID 32219282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32219282/). Meta-analysis (8 RCTs, N=971): combined K+D increased total BMD (ES 0.316) and lowered ucOC, with a more favorable effect for K2. Small pooled surrogate effect, no fractures.
[^S50]: Manson JE et al. 2010, *Menopause*. [PMID 20551849](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20551849/). WHI CaD coronary-calcium substudy (N=754): calcium + D3 400 IU vs placebo over ~7 yr showed NO difference in coronary artery calcium — no signal that supplemental D drives arterial calcification in humans at normal doses.
[^S51]: Haroon SWP et al. 2023, *Kidney Int Rep*. [PMID 37705910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37705910/). Trevasc-HDK (MK-7 360 µg 3x/wk, 18 mo, N=178 hemodialysis): PRIMARY coronary-calcification endpoint NOT different; dp-ucMGP fell. The MK-7 dialysis trial — biomarker moved, calcification didn't.
[^S52]: Holden RM et al. 2023, *Nephrol Dial Transplant*. [PMID 35641194](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35641194/). iPACK-HD (K1 10 mg 3x/wk, 12 mo, N=86 hemodialysis): NO difference in coronary calcification progression; CAC advanced regardless. Cleanest 'fixing the biomarker ≠ fixing the artery' demonstration.
[^S56]: Lee NK et al. 2007, *Cell*. [PMID 17693256](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17693256/). The Karsenty-lab mouse study proposing UNDERcarboxylated osteocalcin as a bone-derived hormone boosting insulin secretion/sensitivity — the mechanistic basis for K2/diabetes and K2/testosterone claims (note: K2 carboxylates osteocalcin, LOWERING the active form the theory credits).
[^S57]: Diegel CR et al. 2020, *PLoS Genet*. [PMID 32463812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32463812/). An osteocalcin-deficient mouse strain WITHOUT endocrine abnormalities — normal glucose metabolism, undercutting the osteocalcin-as-metabolic-hormone rationale.
[^S58]: Moriishi T et al. 2020, *PLoS Genet*. [PMID 32463816](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32463816/). Osteocalcin-KO mice: osteocalcin is necessary for apatite-crystal alignment but NOT for glucose metabolism, TESTOSTERONE SYNTHESIS, or muscle mass. Directly refutes the osteocalcin→testosterone bridge in mice.
[^S60]: Choi HJ et al. 2011, *Diabetes Care*. [PMID 21868771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21868771/). The only K2/insulin-sensitivity RCT: MK-4 30 mg 3x/day, 4 WEEKS, N=42 HEALTHY young men — insulin-sensitivity index rose, tracking reduced ucOC. Tiny, short, surrogate, not diabetics.
[^S63]: Shea MK et al. 2008, *Am J Epidemiol*. [PMID 18006902](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18006902/). Framingham Offspring: higher vitamin-K status associated with lower CRP/IL-6, but the association ATTENUATED after adjustment. Cross-sectional only.
[^S65]: Nimptsch K et al. 2008, *Am J Clin Nutr*. [PMID 18400723](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18400723/). EPIC-Heidelberg (N≈11,319 men): higher dietary MENAQUINONE (not K1) associated with lower prostate cancer (HR 0.65; advanced HR 0.37). Observational, K2 from cheese/dairy.
[^S67]: Habu D et al. 2004, *JAMA*. [PMID 15265851](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15265851/). A small open-label subgroup (~N=40 cirrhotic women): K2 45 mg/day → less hepatocellular carcinoma (RR 0.13). Tiny, open-label, subgroup — the early promise.
[^S68]: Yoshida H et al. 2011, *Hepatology*. [PMID 21574174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21574174/). The large double-blind phase-III RCT of MK-4 45/90 mg for HCC recurrence after ablation — STOPPED AT INTERIM FOR FUTILITY, no benefit. The definitive null that overturned the cancer claim.
[^S69]: Neogi T et al. 2008, *Ann Rheum Dis*. [PMID 18625626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18625626/). Vitamin K (K1) RCT in hand osteoarthritis (N=378): NO overall effect; a subgroup signal only in those K-deficient at baseline. Null overall.
[^S72]: Visser MPJ et al. 2024, *J Clin Med*. [PMID 38930004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38930004/). MK-7 999 µg/day RCT in hospitalised COVID-19 (N=40): improved K status (dp-ucMGP, PIVKA-II) but did NOT change desmosine and was not powered for clinical outcomes.
[^S73]: Theuwissen E et al. 2013, *J Thromb Haemost*. [PMID 23530987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23530987/). The key interaction study: in acenocoumarol-anticoagulated volunteers, MK-7 45 µg/day lowered INR ~40%, and even 10-20 µg/day mattered in 40-60% of subjects. Conclusion: MK-7 supplements must be avoided on VKA therapy.
[^S77]: Caluwé R et al. 2014, *Nephrol Dial Transplant*. [PMID 24285428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24285428/). MK-7 dose-finding in dialysis: dp-ucMGP fell 17%/33%/46% across 360/720/1080 µg 3x/wk — dose-responsive on the biomarker; dropouts were from GI intolerance/odor, not toxicity.
[^S79]: Theuwissen E et al. 2012, *Br J Nutr*. [PMID 22289649](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22289649/). Even low-dose MK-7 (down to ~10-90 µg/day) improved extra-hepatic vitamin-K status (dp-ucMGP, ucOC) without altering thrombin generation.
[^S81]: Indian Pediatr RCT 2025, *Indian Pediatr*. [PMID 39912270](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39912270/). Newborn vitamin-K-deficiency-bleeding prophylaxis uses phytonadione (K1) 1 mg IM — the 'vitamin K shot for babies' is K1, NOT K2 (and never menadione/K3, which was abandoned for neonatal hemolysis).
[^S82]: StatPearls (Eden/Coviello) 2023, *NCBI Bookshelf NBK551578*. [NCBI Bookshelf NBK551578](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK551578/). Menadione (synthetic vitamin K3) is oxidatively toxic — glutathione depletion, hemolytic anemia, kernicterus — and was withdrawn from human use. Its toxicity is distinct from and does NOT transfer to MK-4/MK-7.
[^S83]: Ito A et al. 2011, *Lipids Health Dis*. [PMID 21914161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21914161/). MK-4 (75 mg/kg diet) raised testosterone in RATS and testis-derived tumor cells via CYP11A/PKA — the ONLY source for 'K2 boosts testosterone', and it is rodent/cell, high-dose, with NO human data.
[^S84]: EFSA NDA Panel 2009, *EFSA J (DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2009.1228)*. DOI [10.2903/j.efsa.2009.1228](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2009.1228). EFSA AUTHORIZED the claim that vitamin K contributes to 'maintenance of normal bones' + 'normal blood coagulation' — a nutrient-adequacy (deficiency-avoidance) claim, NOT an endorsement that K2 treats osteoporosis or reduces fractures.
[^S85]: EFSA NDA Panel 2012, *EFSA J (DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2012.2714)*. DOI [10.2903/j.efsa.2012.2714](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2012.2714). EFSA REJECTED the vitamin-K2 'normal function of the heart and blood vessels' health claim (ID 125) on reassessment — so 'K2 protects your arteries' is NOT an authorized EU claim.
