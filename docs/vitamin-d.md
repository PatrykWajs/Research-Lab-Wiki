# Vitamin D: What It Actually Does vs What People Think

> Vitamin D became the "everyone is deficient, megadose to be safe" supplement of the 2010s — sold for bones, immunity, mood, testosterone, cancer and longevity. Which of those are real, how strong is the evidence, and is any of it a benefit of *supplementing a sufficient person* — or is the payoff only ever correcting a genuine deficiency? This is the whole picture, **ranked by how strongly the evidence backs each claim** — settled consensus first, single-study and myth last.

**Complete** · Published 6 August 2026 · *One 8-cluster literature sweep; every one of the 90 cited study PMIDs was independently title-matched against PubMed — 3 wrong PMIDs were caught and corrected, and 8 retraction / Expression-of-Concern notices confirmed (two nonlinear-Mendelian-randomization papers were pulled)* · **90 cited sources** · Confidence: **High** on the primary-prevention nulls, the "deficiency is a cutoff artifact" and reverse-causation story, and the bolus/high-dose harm; **Moderate** on the deficiency-gated wins (mortality in the elderly, type-2 diabetes, pregnancy, respiratory infection) and the exact shape of the cancer-mortality signal.

## Start here

!!! quote "Bottom line"
    **If you are not actually deficient, vitamin D pills do almost nothing for your health, and taking megadoses "to be safe" can quietly hurt you.**

**Myths vs. reality**

| What most people believe | What the evidence actually shows |
|---|---|
| "Almost everyone is deficient, so I probably am too." | A cutoff trick. At the evidence-based line (20 ng/mL) only about **6%** are truly deficient. Move the line to the industry-linked 30 ng/mL and suddenly "70 to 80% are insufficient." |
| "Vitamin D is great for your bones." | In healthy adults it does not cut fractures or raise bone density. The 25,871-person VITAL trial found no fewer fractures, not even in people with low levels, and 10,000 IU a day actually **lost** bone density over 3 years. |
| "It protects your heart and prevents cancer and early death." | Null in the big trials. An 83,000-person analysis put heart-event risk at exactly **1.00**, and cancer and death rates did not move in already-sufficient people. |
| "More is better, so megadose to be safe." | The curve flattens, then bends toward harm. High daily doses lose bone, and a single big annual dose raised falls and fractures. The safe ceiling is **4,000 IU a day**. |
| "Low vitamin D causes all these diseases." | Mostly backwards. Obesity lowers your level, and low D is usually a marker of being unwell, not the cause. The famous study "proving" low D causes heart disease and death was **retracted twice**. |
| "It boosts testosterone, mood, and immunity." | Null for testosterone even in men with genuinely low T, null for depression in an 18,353-person trial, and null for preventing COVID. |
| "Sunbathe to get your vitamin D naturally." | A cheap pill gets you the same blood level with none of the skin-cancer, melanoma, or ageing cost. |
| "It cures winter depression (SAD)." | That claim traces to one 1999 study of 15 people with no placebo group. The large trials show nothing. |

**What you actually learn**

- The "everyone is deficient" panic is a number game: at the honest cutoff only about **6%** of people are truly deficient, but shift the line and it jumps to 70 to 80%.
- The **Endocrine Society reversed its own 2011 guideline in 2024** and now says do NOT routinely test healthy people. The US Preventive Services Task Force agrees.
- The classic "vitamin D for strong bones" idea collapsed. The biggest trial (25,871 people) found no fracture benefit at all, even in the low-level subgroup.
- More is not safer: **10,000 IU a day lost bone density** over 3 years versus a low dose, and big infrequent megadoses increase falls and fractures.
- The celebrated science claiming low vitamin D causally drives heart disease and death was **retracted twice** because the method was broken.
- Being overweight lowers your vitamin D reading, not the other way around, so a low number is often just a sign of poor health you cannot fix with a pill.
- It does not raise testosterone (tested even in low-T men) and does not prevent depression (tested in 18,353 people) or COVID.
- Where it genuinely works is correcting real deficiency: it cures rickets and osteomalacia, cuts fractures in the frail housebound elderly (with calcium), and lifelong sufficiency lowers multiple-sclerosis risk.
- To fix a real deficit, a modest **1,000 to 2,000 IU taken daily** is the sweet spot, under the 4,000 IU/day ceiling. Daily beats big weekly or monthly doses.

!!! tip "What this means for you"
    - If you feel fine and are not in a risk group, do not bother testing or supplementing. A sufficient level gains you almost nothing.
    - Only test your 25(OH)D if you are genuinely at risk: dark skin, housebound, obese, or with gut malabsorption.
    - Taking 5,000 IU "to be safe" with no recent blood test? Dial it down to about 1,000 to 2,000 IU a day unless a test shows you are low. Keep it daily, with K2 and a fatty meal.
    - If you are low, fix it with a modest daily dose, never a big weekly or monthly megadose. Bolus dosing backfires.
    - Do not sunbathe for vitamin D and do not chase 10,000 IU. More costs you bone; it does not add it.

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    Vitamin D is a **real treatment for real deficiency that got mis-sold as a health booster for everyone.** Correcting a genuine deficiency matters — it cures rickets and osteomalacia, cuts fractures in the deficient housebound elderly (with calcium),[^S17] and lifelong sufficiency lowers multiple-sclerosis risk with rare causal support.[^S75] But in already-replete adults, supplementation moves **almost no hard outcome**: it does not prevent heart disease,[^S4] cancer,[^S5] death,[^S1] fractures,[^S12][^S13] falls,[^S24] depression,[^S54] cognitive decline,[^S60] COVID,[^S43] or low testosterone.[^S50][^S51] Past ~1,000–2,000 IU/day the curve flattens then bends toward harm — 10,000 IU/day *lost* bone density in a 3-year trial,[^S22] and bolus megadoses *increase* falls and fractures.[^S20] "Everyone is deficient" is a **cutoff artifact** (~6% truly deficient at the IOM's 20 ng/mL, versus 70–80% "insufficient" at the industry-linked 30 ng/mL),[^S31][^S33] and the **Endocrine Society reversed itself in 2024**, now advising against routine screening of healthy people.[^S27] The honest rule: **test if you're at-risk, correct a real deficit with a modest daily dose, and stop chasing a number if you're already sufficient.**

!!! tip "The picture, ranked by how strongly the evidence agrees"
    - **Tier 1 — strongest consensus:** in replete adults, supplements are null for CVD, cancer incidence, and death;[^S1][^S4][^S5] the classic bone/fall indication *collapsed* (no fracture, fall, or BMD benefit — USPSTF advises against);[^S12][^S13][^S24] "everyone is deficient" is a cutoff artifact and the Endocrine Society reversed itself in 2024;[^S31][^S27] obesity causally *lowers* vitamin D (reverse causation);[^S37] and it does not raise testosterone or prevent depression.[^S50][^S54]
    - **Tier 2 — real but bounded:** the genuine wins are all deficiency-correction or narrow — rickets/osteomalacia and deficient-elderly fractures (with calcium),[^S17] lower MS risk (causal),[^S75] lower incident autoimmune disease (HR 0.78),[^S46] a modest causal blood-pressure drop,[^S77] and a small D3 mortality benefit in the deficient elderly.[^S7]
    - **Tier 3 — deficiency-gated or contested:** type-2-diabetes prevention (deficient subgroup only),[^S85] pregnancy (low-certainty),[^S86] respiratory infection (small, now borderline),[^S39][^S41] and cancer *mortality* (daily dosing hints yes, bolus went the wrong way).[^S5][^S2]
    - **Tier 4 — myths & marketing:** "everyone is deficient, megadose everyone";[^S28] the nonlinear-MR "low-D causes heart disease" proof (**retracted twice**);[^S67][^S69] "more is better" (10,000 IU loses bone);[^S22] "sunbathe for your vitamin D" (same 25(OH)D, skin-cancer cost);[^S90] and "it cures SAD" (one n=15 uncontrolled trial).[^S59]

## What this review covered

An eight-part search swept the peer-reviewed literature across the flagship CVD/cancer/mortality trials, bone/fractures/falls, immune/respiratory/COVID/autoimmune, the deficiency-threshold and reverse-causation question, testosterone/mood/depression, toxicity and upper limits, the Mendelian-randomization/genetics evidence, and special populations (MS, diabetes, pregnancy). **Every candidate PMID — 90 — was then independently title-matched against PubMed**, which caught 3 wrong PMIDs the search agents had supplied (the VITAL-autoimmune trial, the CYP2R1/multiple-sclerosis study, and the COPSAC wheeze RCT), each corrected against the paper's own title and DOI, and confirmed 8 retraction/Expression-of-Concern notices. A recurring honesty note runs through it: the loudest "vitamin D is powerful" claims tend to come from an industry-linked guideline and small deficient-population trials, while the large, independent, publicly-funded mega-trials keep coming back null.

## Tier 1 — The strongest evidence

### In replete adults, supplements don't prevent heart disease, cancer, or death

The flagship primary-prevention trials are large and mostly publicly-funded (vitamin D is generic, so there is no industry money inflating the *positives* here). VITAL (n=25,871, 2000 IU/day) was null on both co-primary endpoints — invasive cancer HR 0.96 and major cardiovascular events HR 0.97[^S1] — and the definitive 21-trial, 83,000-person meta-analysis put cardiovascular events at a risk ratio of **1.00**.[^S4] Cancer *incidence* is unmoved (RR 0.98),[^S5] and all-cause mortality is null in the replete (D-Health, n=21,315, HR 1.04).[^S2] The mortality benefit that does exist is confined to the deficient elderly (see Tier 2).

### The classic bone-and-fall indication collapsed

This is the result that most surprises people. VITAL's fracture ancillary found no reduction in total, hip, or nonvertebral fractures — not even in the low-25(OH)D or low-bone-density subgroups[^S12] — an 81-trial trial-sequential meta-analysis concluded further trials won't change the null and that vitamin D has no meaningful effect on bone density,[^S13][^S15] and DO-HEALTH (adults ≥70) was null across fractures, function, infection, blood pressure and cognition.[^S14] The US Preventive Services Task Force now recommends *against* vitamin D to prevent falls in community-dwelling older adults — a reversal of its earlier stance.[^S24]

### "Almost everyone is deficient" is an artifact of the cutoff you pick

At the IOM's evidence-based 20 ng/mL, true deficiency is ~6% of US adults[^S31] and ~13% of Europeans at <12 ng/mL.[^S32] The **30 ng/mL** cutoff — popularized by a guideline and review whose lead author has extensive vitamin-D-industry ties[^S28] — manufactures the "70–80% insufficient" pandemic.[^S33] The clearest sign of the correction: the **Endocrine Society reversed its own 2011 guideline in 2024**, now recommending against routine 25(OH)D screening in healthy people and abandoning the 30 ng/mL target,[^S27] joining the IOM[^S30] and USPSTF.[^S26] And the causal arrow often runs backwards: Mendelian randomization shows **obesity lowers 25(OH)D** (largely volumetric dilution), not the reverse,[^S37][^S38] and low vitamin D is mostly a *marker* of ill health, not its cause.[^S34]

### It does not raise testosterone, or prevent depression

Two dedicated trials found no testosterone effect — in healthy men with normal T (it even worsened one insulin index)[^S50] and in men with genuinely low T[^S51] — and a pooled meta-analysis is null.[^S53] The whole "sunshine vitamin boosts T" belief traces to one small, deficient, weight-loss-confounded, never-replicated study.[^S52] Mood and cognition are the same story: VITAL-DEP, the largest depression-prevention trial ever run (n=18,353), found no benefit,[^S54] and the VITAL cognition ancillaries were null in healthy elders.[^S60]

## Tier 2 — Real but bounded (all deficiency-correction or narrow)

- **Rickets, osteomalacia, and fractures in the deficient housebound elderly** are the genuine, settled wins: 800 IU D3 **+ calcium** cut hip fractures 43% in institutionalized, deficient, calcium-poor women (Chapuy) — deficiency + calcium + a housebound population, which is why it does *not* generalize to replete community-dwellers.[^S17]
- **Lower multiple-sclerosis risk** — two large cohorts (US military serum OR 0.38 top-vs-bottom quintile)[^S80][^S81] plus Mendelian randomization[^S75] and a CYP2R1 single-gene instrument[^S76] make MS the cleanest "deficiency is genuinely harmful" story. It is about lifelong sufficiency, not treating established MS.
- **Lower incident autoimmune disease** — VITAL's autoimmune ancillary cut new autoimmune disease HR 0.78 over ~5 years,[^S46] the strongest hard-outcome win in the project (it attenuated after supplements stopped, so it may need continuous use).[^S47]
- **A modest, genuinely causal blood-pressure reduction** (Mendelian randomization).[^S77]
- **A modest all-cause-mortality benefit — D3 only, in the deficient/elderly/institutionalized** (Chowdhury RR 0.89; Cochrane), not in replete adults.[^S7][^S8]

## Tier 3 — Deficiency-gated or genuinely contested

- **Type-2 diabetes:** null overall in replete prediabetics (D2d, DPVD),[^S83] with a modest ~15% reduction concentrated in the deficient or those reaching high 25(OH)D on treatment.[^S85]
- **Pregnancy:** probably reduces pre-eclampsia and low birthweight — but at moderate-to-low certainty, and the 2024 Cochrane update downgraded several outcomes.[^S86] Prenatal vitamin D and offspring asthma/wheeze is real-but-borderline (both landmark RCTs missed their primary endpoint).[^S87][^S88]
- **Respiratory infection:** the benefit was always modest (2017 OR 0.88, daily/weekly only, strongest in profound deficiency),[^S39] and after the large null CORONAVIT trial[^S42] and newer studies it **lost significance** entirely in 2025 (OR 0.94, p=0.057).[^S41]
- **Cancer mortality (not incidence):** daily-dosing meta-analyses hint at ~13–16% lower cancer mortality,[^S5][^S6] but monthly-bolus D-Health went the *opposite* way (HR 1.15).[^S2] Unsettled and dosing-dependent.

## Tier 4 — Myths & marketing

- **"Everyone is deficient — test and megadose everyone."** Rests on the contested, industry-linked 30 ng/mL cutoff;[^S28] the IOM,[^S31] USPSTF,[^S26] and the 2024 Endocrine Society[^S27] all reject routine screening of healthy adults.
- **"Mendelian randomization proves low vitamin D causes heart disease and death."** The celebrated "L-shaped benefit at deficiency" was **retracted twice** — the Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology nonlinear-MR paper (retracted and republished with *no causal effect at any level*)[^S67][^S68] and the European Heart Journal cardiovascular paper (retracted 2025)[^S69] — because the residual-stratification method was biased.[^S71] Robust MR is null for CVD, type-2 diabetes, and most cancers.[^S72][^S73]
- **"More is better — megadose 10,000 IU to be safe."** Vitamin D is fat-soluble and stored: high daily doses lost bone density,[^S22] bolus megadoses increased falls and fractures,[^S20][^S21] and the IOM upper limit is 4,000 IU/day.[^S30] The much-feared kidney-stone risk is really the **calcium** co-supplement, not vitamin D alone;[^S18][^S62] and a reverse-J mortality curve shows harm at very high serum too.[^S63]
- **"Sunbathe to get your vitamin D."** The same UVB that makes vitamin D also drives skin carcinogenesis; an oral supplement achieves the identical 25(OH)D with none of the DNA-damage, melanoma, or photoaging cost.[^S90]
- **"Vitamin D cures seasonal affective disorder."** The claim traces to a 1999 study of n=15 with *no placebo arm*;[^S59] the large depression trials are null.[^S54]
- **"Vitamin D toxicity is common."** Frank intoxication needs sustained very high intake or manufacturing errors (e.g. men who took 186,400 and 970,000 IU/day from mislabeled products),[^S65] not typical 1,000–4,000 IU use.

## Who actually benefits

- **People with a genuinely low measured 25(OH)D** — dark skin, housebound, malabsorption, obesity, the institutionalized elderly. Correcting a real deficit is the whole point.[^S17][^S31]
- **The deficient elderly, with calcium** — for fractures, in a deficient/housebound population, not replete community-dwellers.[^S17]
- **Anyone concerned about MS risk** — lifelong sufficiency, not high-dose treatment.[^S75]
- **Pregnant women with low status** — targeted, moderate-certainty benefit.[^S86]
- **Everyone else** (healthy, replete): little to gain from supplementing, a real harm to lose from megadosing or bolus dosing,[^S22][^S20] and no reason to routinely test.[^S27]

## The bottom line

Vitamin D is **neither the cure-all the 2010s made it nor useless.** It is a genuine treatment for genuine deficiency — rickets, osteomalacia, the deficient housebound elderly, lifelong MS-relevant sufficiency — with a couple of real bounded wins (autoimmune-disease incidence, a modest causal blood-pressure effect). But for a healthy, replete adult taking it "to be safe," the strongest evidence says it moves almost no hard outcome — not heart disease, cancer, death, fractures, falls, mood, cognition, COVID, or testosterone — and megadosing or bolus dosing adds real harm. The "everyone is deficient" panic was a cutoff artifact the Endocrine Society itself has now walked back. The evidence-based move is to **correct a measured deficiency with a modest daily dose and otherwise leave a sufficient level alone.**
